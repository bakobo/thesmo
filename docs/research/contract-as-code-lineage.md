### The one-paragraph answer

Your instinct about §1 is right: **the Ricardian contract is the closest structural match, and its author's own later work contains a design recommendation that contradicts the naive reading of it.** Grigg concluded — in print, twice — that prose and code should *not* be unified in one artifact but **joined by mutual cryptographic references**, with **prose holding precedence and the power to delegate precedence to code**. That is a directly adoptable rule for Custos. Your instinct about §4 is also right but for a *sharper* reason than "blockchains need total order": the actual failure is that smart-contract formalisms are written against **"the current state"** rather than **"the evidence I have seen,"** and almost every broken mechanism reduces to that one substitution. §5 is where the live research is, and the headline is that a five-year KIT research programme and a 2025 Kleppmann keynote have already named your hardest problem (non-monotonic revocation under concurrency, "duelling admins") without solving it. And the genuinely unoccupied territory — worth stating as Custos's contribution — is that **the distributed-systems literature has rigorous partial-order semantics with trivial rule languages, and the legal-informatics literature has law-shaped rule languages with no distribution model at all. Nobody has joined them.**

# 1. Ricardian contracts (Ian Grigg, 1995–96 →)

## 1.1 What it is, verified

**Provenance.** Developed by **Ian Grigg** and **Gary Howland** at **Systemics** in 1995–96, as part of **Ricardo**, a bond-trading and asset-transfer system; named for David Ricardo. [S, consistent across Wikipedia, iang.org and Grigg's own retrospective]

Grigg's own account of the motivating problem [V, from the 2016/2022 chapter]:

> "The Ricardian contract finds its origins in a 1995-96 project to digitally trade bonds on the Internet. We found that it was difficult to completely describe bonds to traders, because, unlike say currencies, bonds are often highly but not exactly alike. … Our view was that, as we were designing for an open world unsupported by institutions, **anything less than full information was risky to all participants.**"

> "…a deep dive into the nature of bonds revealed that they were contracts in the legal tradition… Therefore, we reasoned, if we could issue contracts, we could issue bonds. To fully describe such financial instruments, the system should not trade virtual or limited copies of contracts, but should rather **find a way to trade the very contracts themselves.**"

Note the shape of that argument. It is *your* argument: the reason not to store a summary of the law in a database is that a summary is a different artifact from the law, and in an open world without institutional backstops, the difference is the attack surface.

## 1.2 The canonical seven-part definition [V, verbatim from the 2004 paper]

> "a single document that is a) a contract offered by an issuer to holders, b) for a valuable right held by holders, and managed by the issuer, c) easily readable by people (like a contract on paper), d) readable by programs (parsable like a database), e) digitally signed, f) carries the keys and server information, and g) allied with a unique and secure identifier."

## 1.3 The binding mechanism — what is actually load-bearing

This is where the 2004 paper earns its priority status, and where most secondary summaries mislead. Three distinct mechanisms, all [V] from the paper:

**(a) One document, two readers.** The artifact is *a text file*. Programs "convert it into internal forms for searching for **name-value pairs**." There is a section per instrument type (bond, share, currency) and further sections declaring, in program-parsable terms, decimalisation, unit labels and titles. **The machine-facing content is parameters, not logic.** Grigg is explicit and self-limiting about this [V]:

> "We have not gone further in this direction than methods to handle decimals. This is partly for lack of demand, and partly because **it is not clear how a court would treat a computer program presented as a contract.**"

So: *the Ricardian contract is not, and was never, a predicate language.* It is a **cryptographically committed, dual-audience parameter carrier with legal prose in the same bytes.** That is a smaller claim than the folklore, and the gap it leaves is precisely where Custos lives.

**(b) The hash is the identifier, and it appears in every downstream record.** The identifier is a message digest over the whole clearsigned document (SHA-1 in the paper, e.g. `e3b445c2a6d82df81ef46b54d386da23ce8f3775` for Systemics' prepaid-services dollars) [V]. The mechanism that does the real work [V]:

> "The hash within the record cannot be changed without losing its ability to pass a test of signature validity. Likewise, the contract cannot be changed without losing its relationship to records already signed and delivered."

And the threat it defeats, named [V]: **"frog-boiling"** — gradual surreptitious amendment — is impossible because "every record, held by every user, **incorporates an unalterable copy of that hash.**"

> **Directly adoptable for Custos.** Every judgment record — every verdict, every conviction, every issued artifact — should carry the digest of *the law it was judged under*, not merely a pointer to "the domain's law." That converts "which version governed at this position?" from administrative testimony into a byte comparison, and it is a *transitive* defence: an adversary who amends the law cannot retroactively re-characterise past judgments, because every past record commits to the old digest. Your philosophy companion already identifies "which version governed at this position is administrative testimony" as the failure of digest-anchored governance-framework documents; Grigg's answer is that the anchoring must be **downstream-replicated into every dependent record**, not just upstream-anchored once.

**(c) Amendment is replacement, and that is deliberate.** [V] "any change to the contract creates a new hash, and that new hash is not the one which the users have or value." No versioning mechanism. Grigg concedes the cost: "it can be a nuisance for the support team of that financial instrument." **This is the design's central limitation for Custos and the reason your `r`-field-only rung fails**: immutability-by-hash gives you integrity at the price of amendability, and amending a rule embedded in N artifacts requires N re-issuances.

## 1.4 The Ricardian triple — attribution and content

The triple is `{prose, parameters, code}`, where the parameters particularise *both* the prose and the code so that one template plus one parameter set yields one deal. [S, consistent across sources]

**Attribution is contested and you should state it carefully.** Grigg introduced a bundling of `{code, prose, params}` in "The Sum of All Chains — Let's Converge!" (29 Apr 2015) [V — I fetched it]. The presentation's framing is that all "First Class Persons" on a chain — humans, corporations, smart contracts, DAOs, machines, issuances — need the same packaging; verbatim [V]: *"it has to contain text and code at the same time… it has to include its own PKI… it has to be able to sign things in order to show intent."* **The term "Ricardian triple" itself is credited by Gabriel Shapiro (MetaLeX, 13 Dec 2024) to Clack, Bakshi & Braine, not to Grigg** [V, fetched <https://metalex.substack.com/p/ricardian-triplers>]. Wikipedia attributes the refactoring to Grigg 2015 [S]. Safest formulation: *the triple's content is Grigg's (2015); the name is Clack/Bakshi/Braine's usage (2016).*

One caution on the 2015 presentation: despite the title, **it contains no argument about consensus versus convergence and no claim about avoiding a global chain** [V — I looked for exactly this and it is not there]. "Converge" there means "let all these entity types converge on one archetype." Do not cite it as philosophical support for Custos's convergence commitment; the resonance is coincidental.

### The R3 / Barclays line — Clack, Bakshi & Braine

- **"Smart Contract Templates: foundations, design landscape and research directions"** — Christopher D. Clack (UCL), Vikram A. Bakshi, Lee Braine (Barclays), arXiv:1608.00771 (Aug 2016, rev. 2017) [S]
- **"Smart Contract Templates: essential requirements and design options"** — same authors, arXiv:1612.04496 (14 Dec 2016) [V for authors/date/abstract; **the body is [S] — I could not extract the PDF text reliably, so treat the detailed claims below as second-hand**]

Their construction [S]: **templates** are electronic representations of legal documents containing prose plus **parameters (identifier, type, optional value)**; **agreements** are fully-instantiated templates; selecting appropriate code yields a Ricardian triple. They distinguish **"execution parameters"** — operational values extracted from the prose and passed to the code — from parameters that are purely legal, and one execution parameter may serve as **the identifier for reference and recovery of the smart legal agreement**.

> **What this supplies that you lack:** the *typed-parameter* discipline. A parameter is (identifier, type, value) and the *same* identifier appears in the prose and in the code. That is a cheap, strong integrity property: a mismatch is detectable statically. Custos's committed predicates should share identifiers with the prose that authorises them, and the sharing should be schema-checked, not conventional. **Verify against the PDF body before citing normatively** — I did not read it.

## 1.5 What Grigg concluded after twenty years — the most valuable finding in this report

From "On the Intersection of Ricardian and Smart Contracts" [all V, verbatim]:

**(a) Do not try to unify prose and code in one artifact. Join them by mutual hash references.**

> "Clack et al (2017) discuss overarching ways to generate code and prose elements from one source document but **prose and code are so far apart, semantically, that treating them as one document remains an open research project for the foreseeable future**… More likely, separated code and prose objects can be authored together and then **joined by means of references (secure addresses such as hashes or blockchain transaction IDs) from one to the other; preferably with both pointing to each other, in mutual form.** This is trivially accompanied by signing off on the prose with the address of the smart code embedded, and then posting the address of the prose Ricardian contract directly into a running smart contract."

This is the inventor of the "one document that is both" design, in his most recent treatment of it, **recommending against one document.** For Custos: a committed predicate and its authorising prose should be *two* SAID-addressed objects with reciprocal digests, not one blended artifact. That also happens to be the only arrangement in which the predicate can be evaluated without parsing legal prose, and the prose can be read in court without parsing predicates.

**(b) Prose leads, and can delegate leadership to code. Code cannot delegate anything.**

> "…if there are two artefacts that present themselves as important parts of the lifecycle of the contract, which leads in the event of mismatch or collision in events? By way of logic, **it is the Ricardian contract**. Fundamentally, the final word on what the contract is falls to the court. But the court is human, and cannot typically read code… Secondly, as prose, it can state that it is the contract, and further it can state that the terms of the code dominate, or otherwise; **whereas code cannot state any sort of thing. Thus, the Ricardian prose leads, because it has the flexibility to pass leadership to the code, when the code is mute on matters outside its mechanical domain.**"

> **Directly adoptable.** This is a complete, defensible **precedence rule** for a governance standard, and it is asymmetric in exactly the way that makes it implementable: prose is the default authority; a prose clause may *elect* to make a named predicate dispositive within a named domain; a predicate can never elect anything. Custos should state this once, normatively, and it removes an entire class of "which one wins" review objections. Note it also resolves the retroactivity question in the honest direction: a predicate that produced a judgment the prose does not sanction is a *bug in the law*, remediable by the amendment procedure, not a valid judgment.

**(c) There is a third phase, and neither design covers it: dispute resolution.**

> "…if we treat the contrast between the two as different phases of the contractual life cycle, then **a third phase is missing** and may demand some comment: dispute resolution… most contracts, say the 99%, will complete without trouble. Yet some small proportion will end up in trouble, and a dispute arises."

His suggested mechanism is a *parameter* [V]: `Dispute_Resolution = "Courts of London, England, to English Law."` — or "a secure link such as the hash of an online Forum's Rules for Arbitration in Ricardian format." **Note that this is structurally identical to Custos's problem of a domain declaring its own convictability semantics, and Grigg's answer is: a committed reference to another committed rule-set.** Which is to say: a jurisdiction. Your philosophy companion's line — *"A Ricardian contract needs a jurisdiction. The rules section is the contract; the GEL is the legal system"* — is not merely compatible with Grigg; **it is the gap Grigg identified and left open.** That is a citable, load-bearing endorsement of Custos's central move by the author of the artifact it extends. I would put it in the spec's related-work section.

**(d) What Grigg says the smart contract actually is:** [V] "computer scientists might prefer to recognise this as **a state machine with money**." And the crowdfunding worked example is captioned **"The events and actions of a state machine."** Worth knowing because it means the *cautionary* section 4 is arguing with a state-machine model that Grigg himself endorses for *performance* while denying it for *semantics*.

## 1.7 The KERI/ToIP lineage — verified, and it matters more than you might expect

**Grigg's concept is already normatively inside the stack Custos builds on.** I fetched the ACDC specification body (`trustoverip/kswg-acdc-specification`, `spec/spec-body.md`, 422 KB) and grepped it. All of the following are **[V], verbatim**:

- Reserved field label table: <code>|`l`| Legal Language| Text of Ricardian contract clause.|</code>
- §"Rule Section": *"The purpose of the Rules section is to provide a set of rules or conditions as a **Ricardian Contract** [43]. The important features of a Ricardian contract are that it is both human and machine-readable and referenceable by a cryptographic digest. A JSON-encoded document or block, such as the Rules section block, is a practical example of both a human and machine-readable document. The Rules section's top-level SAID, `d` field provides the digest. This provision supports the **bow-tie model** of RC."*
- On SAID hierarchies: *"The combination of non-repudiable commitments and hierarchical commitments enables reasoning about ACDCs in whole or in part via their SAIDs… This also supports the well-known **bow-tie model of Ricardian Contracts**."*
- On IPEX: the exchange protocol *"leverages… Ricardian Contracts [43] and Graduated Disclosure… to enable Contractually Protected Disclosure."*
- Reference [43] resolves to **the Wikipedia article on Ricardian contracts** — i.e. the spec cites the concept, not Grigg's paper.
- Editors: **Kevin Griffin (GLEIF)**; authors **Samuel Smith (ProSapien), Philip Feairheller (GLEIF)**; v1.1 [V].
- WebOfTrust glossary (KERISSE) entry for "Ricardian contract" credits **"Ian Grigg in 1996"** and says: *"Ricardian contracts provide a human readable twin to the seals and signatures (commitments) in binary format in ACDC."* [V, fetched]

**Grigg is not an author or acknowledged contributor of the ACDC/KERI drafts** [V — I grepped the spec body for "Grigg" and got zero hits outside the reference list, which itself points at Wikipedia]. His bio lists R3, EOS, Mattereum, Knabu and "innovations in social savings and identity" [V, 2022 chapter] but no KERI/ToIP role surfaced in any source I checked. **So the lineage is conceptual and one-directional: KERI adopted the Ricardian idea; Grigg did not co-design KERI.** If you want a personal connection, it must be established, not assumed.

**Now the finding that actually matters.** I read the full Rule Section specification [V]. Its structure is:

- `r` is a tree of **Rule-groups** (intermediate nodes) and **Rules** (terminal nodes).
- A Rule-group's reserved fields are exactly `[d, u, l]` — SAID, salty nonce, legal language.
- **"A Rule MUST have a Legal, `l`, field… A Rule MUST NOT have any other fields."**
- **"The legal language, `l`, field in each clause block provides the associated legal language as a string."**
- Every Rule and Rule-group may carry its own SAID, giving clause-grade global addressing; adding a high-entropy `u` nonce makes a clause *blinded by its SAID* and therefore selectively disclosable ("private compact clause").
- The whole section MUST be `oneOf`-composable down to just its SAID.
- Discovery of a compacted rule is by SAID → OOBI → service endpoint, or by issuance attachment ("Percolated Discovery").

**Therefore: in ACDC, "machine readable" means JSON-parseable and digest-referenceable. It does not mean machine-evaluable. The `l` field is an opaque prose string. There is no predicate, no parameter typing, no evaluation semantics — not even Grigg's 2004 name-value pairs.** ACDC is *less* machine-facing than the 1996 design in this respect, while being far more sophisticated about addressing, compaction and disclosure.

> **What this means for Custos, concretely.** (1) You are not competing with a solved problem; the substrate's Ricardian claim is an addressing-and-disclosure claim, not a computation claim. (2) The rule tree is nonetheless an excellent *host*: clause-grade SAIDs, hierarchical commitment, selective disclosure via salty nonce, and compaction to a digest are exactly the properties a committed predicate needs. A Custos predicate can be a sibling of `l` inside the existing Rule block shape — or better, per §1.5(a), a *separate* SAID-addressed object that the Rule's prose references and that references the Rule back. (3) The `oneOf`-to-SAID compaction rule is a gift: a verifier that only needs to know *which* predicate governed can carry the digest and fetch the predicate lazily. (4) **The one thing to be careful about:** `u`-blinded private rules mean two verifiers may hold *different subsets of the same rule tree* and both verify. That is a split-view surface at clause granularity, and it interacts badly with "every stranger recomputes the same judgment." Custos should say explicitly whether domain-law predicates may be blinded, and if so, what a judgment computed over a partially-disclosed rule tree even means. I did not find this addressed anywhere.

## 1.8 Verdict on §1

**Supplies:** (i) the hash-in-every-downstream-record discipline and the "frog-boiling" threat model [§1.3b]; (ii) the mutual-reference architecture instead of one blended artifact [§1.5a]; (iii) an asymmetric, defensible prose-leads precedence rule [§1.5b]; (iv) the typed-shared-parameter discipline from Clack et al.; (v) an endorsement-by-gap of Custos's "contract needs a jurisdiction" thesis [§1.5c]; (vi) an existing normative hook in ACDC's `r` section with clause-grade SAIDs and selective disclosure [§1.7].

**Does not supply, and never did:** any predicate language, any evaluation semantics, any amendment mechanism (amendment = new hash = new contract), any notion of an observer, any notion of a partial order or of incomplete evidence, any notion of a rule applying to a *class* of parties over *time*. Grigg's contracts are per-instrument, issuer-signed, and static by construction.

## 2.3 Ergo's computational model

**Is it a state machine over contract state? Effectively yes — it is a fold, and the CLI proves it.** From the archived README [V, verbatim]:

```
ergo draft       create a contract text from data
ergo trigger     send a request to the contract
ergo invoke      invoke a clause of the contract
ergo initialize  initialize the state for a contract
ergo compile     compile a contract
```

`initialize` + `trigger` is an explicit `(state, request) → (state', response, emits)` discipline: state is *initialised* once and *threaded* through triggers, with obligations/events emitted. That is precisely the shape of a governance fold, and the CLI verbs are close to what a Custos reference implementation would want (`initialize`, `trigger`, `draft`).

**Typing and formal basis** [V for the compiler facts from the README, S for the design-goal bullets from the docs]:

- [V] "The Ergo compiler is **written using the Coq proof assistant**, with parsing and support code written in OCaml. It makes extensive use of the **Q\*cert compiler** for code generation and type checking." Packages: `ergo-cli`, `ergo-compiler`, `ergo-engine` (Node VM), `ergo-test` (Cucumber).
- [V] "Both the Ergo language and its compiler are in **early development phase**" — the README's own self-assessment, at archival.
- [S] Strongly typed; types come from **Concerto** models; "clauses have a well-defined type signature (input and output), they are functions **without side effects**"; "the compiler **guarantees error-free execution for well-typed Ergo programs**"; "clauses and functions are written in an expression language with **limited expressiveness (it allows conditional and bounded iteration)**"; design goal: "**prevent run-time errors and non-terminating logic**"; design goal: **blockchain neutral**; design goal: **formally specified**, "so the meaning of contracts is well defined and can be verified and preserved during execution."

> **What Ergo supplies that you lack — and it is a lot, despite being dead.** (i) **A worked answer to "must a governance predicate language be total?"** — yes, and the way to get it is a restricted expression language with *conditional and bounded iteration only*, no general recursion, no fixpoints. That is a directly copyable language-design constraint, and it is the same conclusion Catala reaches from the legal side (§5). (ii) **The evidence that a legal-logic DSL can be mechanised in Coq** — the compiler *was* written in Coq, which is a real existence proof for "the fold's semantics can be machine-checked," and a stronger one than anything in the CRDT literature had until 2026. (iii) **The `(state, request) → (state', response, obligations)` clause signature with pure clauses** — steal the shape. (iv) **Blockchain-neutrality as an explicit, published design goal in a legal-contract language** — useful citable precedent that this is a legitimate stance, not an eccentricity.

## 2.4 What the project learned or abandoned — the most instructive datapoint in §2

Ergo is archived and the logic layer is now plain TypeScript. There is no dedicated deprecation post [V — I looked and did not find one], but the current docs make the reasoning legible [S]:

1. **Ecosystem size.** TypeScript is "a strongly-typed, general purpose programming language supported by a vibrant open source and enterprise community"; Ergo needed a bespoke compiler and toolchain (in Coq/OCaml — a maintenance burden borne by very few people).
2. **The type-safety goal was achievable without the DSL.** Templates are "statically compiled to TypeScript programs, enforcing type-safety and ensuring **no unsafe `eval` is required at runtime**." Concerto supplies the types; the host language supplies execution. Ergo compiled to JavaScript anyway, so the DSL was an intermediate layer that could be removed.
3. **LLM alignment (the 2025–26 motivation).** "Agents write TypeScript fluently; they don't know Ergo." The `apap` repo (Agreement Runtime + MCP server) confirms this is now a first-class concern [V].

> **The lesson for Custos, stated plainly.** Custos's predicate language will be under continuous pressure to become "a subset of a popular language plus a schema." That pressure won here, in the most serious, best-funded, Linux-Foundation-hosted attempt at exactly this, after six years, *despite* the DSL having a Coq-mechanised compiler and formal termination guarantees.
>
> **But the pressure should not win for Custos, and the reason is specific.** Accord Project contracts are executed by *one party's* runtime, for *that party's* purposes, with the other party's recourse being a court reading the prose. Custos's premise is the opposite: **a stranger must recompute the identical judgment.** Under that premise, "compile to TypeScript" is fatal — TypeScript is not a specification, its semantics are not total, and two conformant implementations can differ. **Accord Project could give up the DSL because it never needed byte-identical third-party replay; Custos cannot, because that is its whole product.** That is the argument to have ready, because it is the first thing a reviewer who knows Accord Project will say. And the corollary: if you *do* define a DSL, it must be small enough that a second implementation is cheap, or the same pressure will kill it — which is an argument for Ergo's *restriction* (conditional + bounded iteration, no recursion) rather than Ergo's *ambition*.

## 2.5 Also worth knowing

**Concerto** [V, alive: 200 stars, pushed 2026-07-13] is the surviving, load-bearing piece: a strongly-typed schema language with a metamodel, CLI, conformance suite, codegen, Rust/.NET implementations, and a tree-sitter grammar. If Custos needs a *typed data model* for the facts its predicates range over — as opposed to inventing one — Concerto is a mature, ledger-neutral, legal-domain-tested candidate, and it is what Accord Project kept when it threw the DSL away. That is a meaningful signal about which half of "types + logic" is the durable half.

# 3. OpenLaw and other markup-plus-logic legal systems

## 3.1 OpenLaw — dead; useful only as a cautionary datapoint

**What/who:** ConsenSys-incubated, founded 2017 by **Aaron Wright** (Cardozo Law, Blockchain Project) and **David Roon**. A "Legal Markup" language — wiki-text-like — turning natural-language agreements into machine-readable templates with typed variables (`[[Party Name]]`, `[[Confirm: YesNo]]`), composable **Clauses** assembled "like Lego blocks," and email-based e-signature markup. Ethereum-integrated. [S]

**Status: dead, verified.** [V, GitHub API]
- `openlawteam/openlaw-core` — **archived**, last push **2021-08-03**, 111 stars, **Scala**.
- `openlawteam/openlaw-client` — archived, 2023-01-06. `openlaw-website` — archived, 2023-01-11.
- The org renamed to **TributeLabs** and pivoted to DAO tooling (announced by Wright at Mainnet 2021 [S]); `tribute-contracts` (298 stars) is itself now **archived**, last push 2024-10-17 [V]. Of the org's recent repos, only `gate` (an AI PR-review tool) and `ape-ledger` are unarchived.

So the trajectory was: legal markup language → DAO framework → DAO framework abandoned. **Both halves failed.**

**What it would supply:** essentially nothing you cannot get better elsewhere. Its variable-in-prose syntax is TemplateMark's less-developed cousin; its logic was Ethereum-coupled; its identifier model was not hash-based. **Its one transferable lesson is negative and worth stating:** OpenLaw bound prose to *deployed on-chain code* and to a hosted service, and when the company's business model moved, the artifacts became unverifiable. Custos's equivalent risk is binding predicates to a particular engine implementation rather than to a specified semantics.

## 3.2 CommonAccord (James Hazard) — alive, and the best prior art for *composition*

**What it is** [S]: a "data-model for text — an extremely simple and expandable data-model consisting of a series of nested lists that render into texts." Units are **"prose objects."** The goal is to make documents "so modular that much of the text disappears, leaving parties with only specific deal points." Explicitly framed as open-sourcing a legal code — "fulfilling the goals of the Uniform Commercial Code and Napoleonic Code by open source methods," and "to lawyers what GitHub is for software developers."

**Who:** **James Hazard**, with Marc Dangeard, **Thomas Hardjono** (MIT Connection Science), and Primavera De Filippi (Berkman Klein). W3C blockchain-workshop position paper, 2016: <https://www.w3.org/2016/04/blockchain-workshop/interest/hazard-hardjono.html> [S].

**Status: alive.** [V, GitHub API] Org `CommonAccord`, 223 repos. `Cmacc-Org` pushed **2026-07-23** (48 stars). Recent instantiations: YC SAFE (2026-04), ABA Model Stock Purchase Agreement (2026-03), EU-INC / EU-CSDDD (2026-01/02). Implementation is a PHP front end over a Perl parser (`parser.pl`) that expands fields across local and remote documents [S] — i.e. **the engineering is fragile hobby-grade, but the *content corpus* and the *model* are real and maintained.**

**Why it matters, per Grigg** [V, from the 2022 chapter]: *"CommonAccord places small smart contracts with Ricardians and then composes these pairs into larger agreements"*, illustrated by his Figure 9, captioned **"Coupling code snippets to clauses and composing upwards."**

> **What CommonAccord supplies that you lack: the answer to Grigg's 2004 "layering" problem, and it is the one thing in this entire report that addresses *composition of law* rather than *evaluation of law*.** Custos's domains will have rules that derive from other rules, incorporate standard terms by reference, and specialise general norms. CommonAccord's model — a norm is a *reference to a named prose object plus a set of overrides*, resolved by expansion — is a working, decade-tested design for that, and it composes *pairs* of (prose, code) upward rather than composing prose and code separately. Combined with ACDC's per-rule SAIDs, you get content-addressed prose objects with clause-grade references, which is a strictly better substrate than CommonAccord's own file paths.
>
> Two cautions. (1) Expansion-based composition means **the law you evaluate is a *derived* artifact**; Custos must decide whether the committed thing is the reference graph or the expansion, and must commit to *both* digests if it wants replayability (the expansion is what was judged; the graph is what was legislated). (2) CommonAccord has no notion of a partial order, an observer, or amendment-over-time.

**Bonus lead:** `CommonAccord/on-agreements-paper` — **"Reimagining Agreements," Michael Zargham & James Hazard**, draft, pushed 2025-04-23 [V, I read the README]. Its three-domain ontology is directly usable framing:

| Domain [V, verbatim table] | Native Form | Primary Concern | Legibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Relationships** | Verbal/social agreements | Intent, trust, good faith | Legible to humans |
| **Processes** | Software, operational logic | Execution, automation, enforcement | Legible to machines |
| **Legal Glue** | Contracts, regulations | Interpretation, recourse, legitimacy | Legible to legal systems |

And their conditions for a template to become "a source of confidence" [V, verbatim, lightly condensed]: it "has been reused widely and is recognized by name (**ideally, also a unique identifier like a git hash**)"; "it is easy to **verify that a given document conforms to that template** (such as using checksums)"; "its parameters—or ProseObjects—are **well-typed and clearly defined**"; "it is maintained by professionals whose roles and licenses carry accountability." That is a four-point conformance argument for exactly the artifact Custos wants to standardise, written by a governance researcher and a legal-code practitioner, and it independently reproduces Grigg's hash-identity requirement and Clack et al.'s typed-parameter requirement.

**Michael Zargham / BlockScience** more broadly [S]: "Aligning 'decentralized autonomous organization' to precedents in cybernetics" (Zargham & Nabben, *MIT Computational Law Report*, 2023); "What constitutes a constitution?" (Zargham, Alston, Nabben, Ben-Meir, 2023); "Algorithms as Policy" (Zargham & Nabben, 2020); "Method for functional decomposition of organizations and their environments" (Zenodo, 2023). This is *institutional-analysis* work, not predicate-language work — relevant to how you justify Custos's governance model to a policy audience, not to how you design the fold. Note the emphasis on **"the subjectivity of the governance designer"**, which is a friendly frame for observer-relative judgment.

## 3.3 Stipula and Symboleo — the academic state of the art in legal contract languages

Both are [S] — from search summaries; I did not fetch the papers. Both are worth someone actually reading.

**Stipula** — **Silvia Crafa, Cosimo Laneve, Giovanni Sartor** (Padova/Bologna). Papers: *"Pacta sunt servanda: legal contracts in Stipula,"* arXiv:2110.11069, *Science of Computer Programming* 2022; *"Programming Legal Contracts,"* Springer LNCS 2022; *"Stipula: a domain specific language for legal contracts,"* ProLaLa@POPL 2022 (<https://www.math.unipd.it/~crafa/Pubblicazioni/ProLaLa22.pdf>); platform paper at <https://cs.unibo.it/~laneve/papers/StipulaPlatform.pdf>.

Design [S]: derived from a legal-theoretic analysis distilling four features — **agreement, permissions, violations, obligations**. Explicitly and pervasively **state-machine oriented**: "function definitions specify state pre- and post-conditions **plus which party is authorized to call the function**"; permissions/empowerments = the possibility of acting at a given stage; prohibitions = interdiction; **obligations are recast as commitments checked at a time limit**. Distinct syntax for value updates vs **linear asset transfers**. Analyses: type inference for fields/assets/functions; a **liquidity analyser** that finds contracts freezing assets forever; and recent **deductive verification** by translation to JML-annotated Java proved with **KeY**, using a "dispatch table" to represent event-driven and time-dependent behaviour statically (see also *"Formal Verification of Legal Contracts: A Translation-based Approach,"* arXiv:2509.20421, and *"Deductive Verification of Legal Contracts,"* Springer 2025).

> **Supplies:** the cleanest published mapping from *deontic* concepts to *executable* ones — `(state, caller-authorisation, precondition) → (state', effects)` with obligations as time-bounded commitments. That is the exact shape of a Custos governance predicate, and the "which party is authorized to call" being part of the function signature rather than a body check is a good discipline. Also: a language whose obligations are *checked at a deadline* rather than *enforced at an instant*, which is what you need when there is no global clock — an obligation whose violation is convictable only after a deadline observable in the log is order-insensitive in a way that an instantaneous check is not.

**Symboleo** — **Sharifi, Parvizimosaed, Amyot, Logrippo, Mylopoulos** et al. (University of Ottawa). *"Symboleo: Towards a Specification Language for Legal Contracts,"* RE 2020, IEEE doi 9218159; Parvizimosaed's PhD thesis (uOttawa); SymboleoPC (model checking), Symboleo2SC (→ Hyperledger Fabric), SymboleoAC (RBAC extension).

Design [S]: a **requirements-engineering** framing — contract terms *are* requirements. Contracts are collections of **obligations** (a kind of duty) and **powers** (a kind of right) that define the contract's compliant executions. Formal semantics = **logical axioms over statecharts** describing the lifetimes of contracts, obligations and powers. Grounded in the **Unified Foundational Ontology** and its legal extension **UFO-L**. Verification in **LTL/CTL**. Supports runtime **subcontracting, assignment of rights, substitution of performance**.

> **Supplies:** (i) **obligations and powers as first-class entities with their own lifecycles**, each a state machine constrained by axioms — which is a materially better decomposition than "one state machine for the whole contract" when different obligations progress independently, i.e. **concurrently, i.e. in a partial order**. Symboleo is the closest thing in the legal-DSL literature to admitting concurrency. (ii) A grounded ontology (UFO-L) so you are not inventing "role," "power," "obligation," "asset" from scratch. (iii) Runtime delegation primitives (assignment, substitution) that a governance standard will eventually need and that no capability system frames legally.

**Comparison, for your purposes:** Stipula is an executable DSL with linear assets and termination-oriented analyses; Symboleo is a declarative specification language with an ontology and model checking. **Custos wants Symboleo's decomposition (independent obligation/power lifecycles) with Stipula's evaluation discipline (typed, authorised, terminating) — and neither has any distribution model.**

# 4. Smart-contract and DAO governance languages — testing the cautionary expectation

*This section was produced by a delegated researcher; markings are theirs, normalised. Three caveats on its evidence are recorded at §4.9.*

## 4.0 Your expectation is mostly right, but the reason is wrong — and the correction sharpens the design

**The problem is not "smart contracts require a total order." It is that smart-contract formalisms require *a uniquely-named current state*, and they obtain that name from a total order.**

That reframing matters because there is a **necessary-and-sufficient theory** of exactly which predicates need it, and there are production systems that deliberately drop consensus for the subset that does not. Sui, FastPay, Linera and Radix are real counterexamples — but each keeps consensus as a fallback, and each confines the consensusless path to **single-writer state.** That boundary is the most useful thing in this section.

**A second, more important correction to the framing, because it will sharpen the whole design:**

> **Custos is not a "no total order" system.** A KERI KEL *is* a total order — a local one, self-certified by each event naming its predecessor's digest. What Custos lacks is only three things: (1) a total order *across independent logs*; (2) a guarantee that a given log has no fork (duplicity is *detectable*, not *preventable*); (3) a shared clock.
>
> So the failure surface is not sequencing. Intra-log sequencing is free and exact. The failure surface is **(a) joins across logs, (b) branch selection under duplicity, and (c) the absence of a shared clock.** Every broken mechanism in §4.4 reduces to one of those three.

And the general form of the fix:

> EVM predicates have the shape `P(σ)` where `σ` is *the* global state. Custos predicates must have the shape `P(E)` where `E` is the evidence the evaluator happens to hold. For `P` to be well-defined across strangers you need one of:
> - **Monotone in E** — more evidence never flips true to false. (Then all honest observers eventually agree, and disagreement is only ever "not yet.")
> - **Named prefix** — `P` reads a *causally-closed prefix identified by a digest*. **This is the killer move.** "The roster as of event digest D" is exactly as deterministic as an EVM storage read, because the digest pins the state. **Content-addressing gives you back the thing consensus was buying.**
>
> `P` is ill-defined precisely when it must read a prefix **nobody can name yet** — e.g. "the set of all votes cast by deadline T," because T is a wall-clock, not a digest.

## 4.1 The theoretical spine — read this before anything else in §4

### Kleppmann & Howard, "Byzantine Eventual Consistency and the Fundamental Limits of Peer-to-Peer Databases" [V]
arXiv:2012.00472, 1 Dec 2020. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00472> · full text read via <https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.00472>

**This is the single most relevant paper to Custos in this report and it should be cited in the design rationale.**

**BEC is defined by seven properties** (verbatim or near-verbatim): *self-update*; *eventual update* ("For any update applied by a correct replica, all correct replicas will eventually apply that update"); ***convergence*** ("Any two correct replicas that have applied **the same set of updates** are in the same state"); *atomicity*; *authenticity* ("If a correct replica applies an update that is labeled as originating from replica s, then that update was generated by replica s"); *causal consistency*; *invariant preservation*. **Note that convergence is stated over *sets* of updates, not orders — that is Custos's claim, formalised.**

**Theorem 3.1:** *"There exists a fault-tolerant algorithm that ensures BEC **if and only if** the set of all transactions executed by correct replicas is **ℐ-confluent** with respect to each of the invariants."*

**What it forbids, in the paper's own examples:**
- Uniqueness: "If two transactions T₁ and T₂ both create data items with the same value in that attribute, then {T₁,T₂} is not ℐ-confluent: each of T₁ and T₂ individually preserves the constraint, but the combination does not."
- Budgets: "If T₁ and T₂ both decrease the same user's account balance, then they are not ℐ-confluent." Therefore "a cryptocurrency cannot be immune to Sybil attacks" and "needs Sybil countermeasures such as proof-of-work."

**What it permits:** ℐ-confluent applications "can be implemented in a permissionless peer-to-peer system **without resorting to proof-of-work**," including many CRDT applications and "most of the transactions and invariants in the TPC-C benchmark."

**Mechanism:** Byzantine causal broadcast over a hash-chained message DAG; messages are triples `(v, hs, sig)` where `hs` are hashes of causal predecessors; reconciliation exchanges only undelivered messages using **"heads"** as summaries; delivery by topological sort. **Structurally the same shape as a KERI KEL plus receipts — a strong architectural validation.**

> **Supplies: an actual decision procedure.** For every governance predicate you plan to admit, ask whether it is ℐ-confluent w.r.t. the invariants it protects. If not, you must either partition the state so only one author can violate it, or change the policy. **This should be a documented design gate in the Custos spec, not an afterthought.**

### Bailis, Fekete, Franklin, Ghodsi, Hellerstein, Stoica, "Coordination Avoidance in Database Systems" [S]
arXiv:1402.2237, VLDB 2014, doi:10.14778/2735508.2735509. Origin of I-confluence: "an application invariant can be preserved without coordination iff it is I-confluent — merging any two invariant-preserving states yields an invariant-preserving state." **The nuance that matters: the test depends on both the transaction *and* the invariant.** `Balance > 100` is I-confluent under deposits but not withdrawals; `Balance < 100` is the reverse. So *"is this governance rule safe without consensus" is never a property of the rule alone — it is a property of (rule, admissible event types).*

### Kleppmann, "Making CRDTs Byzantine Fault Tolerant" (PaPoC@EuroSys 2022) [S], with a caveat
doi:10.1145/3517209.3524042. Per search extracts: standard CRDTs **do not** converge under Byzantine faults even with eventual delivery, because "divergence arises when replicas make inconsistent decisions about the validity of updates"; the fix is to **make the validity decision depend only on the causal past of the update itself**, regardless of concurrent updates. Tolerates *any number* of Byzantine nodes.

**⚠ Caveat, flagged by the researcher:** a direct fetch of `martin.kleppmann.com/papers/bft-crdt-papoc22.pdf` produced a summary claiming "f of 3f+1" tolerance, which **contradicts** both the search extract and the BEC paper's thesis. **Judged to be a bad PDF summarisation. Do not rely on it; verify directly before citing.**

> The design rule that falls out is the single most actionable line in §4: **a Custos predicate must be evaluable from the causal past of the event being judged, never from the set of events concurrent with it.**

### Almeida & Shapiro, "The Blocklace: A Byzantine-repelling and Universal Conflict-free Replicated Data Type" [V]
arXiv:2402.08068, Feb 2024, rev. Jan 2025. "The blocklace is a **partially-ordered generalization of the blockchain** in which each block has any finite number of signed hash pointers to preceding blocks." It can "detect and eventually exclude Byzantine nodes, including equivocators," and "ensures that **a Byzantine node may harm only a finite prefix of the computation.**" Claimed as a universal BFT implementation for arbitrary op-based CRDTs.

> **Supplies: a *target theorem* for Custos duplicity handling** — not "duplicity is impossible" but "duplicity harms only a finite prefix, and equivocators are eventually excluded." That is defensible and achievable.

## 4.2 The languages: what is worth knowing

### Solidity / EVM and its verification stack [S unless noted]
- **KEVM / K framework** (Runtime Verification, from Grigore Roșu's FSL at UIUC): complete formal EVM semantics in K, passes the full Ethereum test suite (40,000+ programs). <https://jellopaper.org/> · <https://github.com/runtimeverification/evm-semantics>. Current products **Kontrol** (KEVM + Foundry) and **Simbolik**; clients EigenLayer, Optimism, Uniswap, Lido, Polkadot. **Important negative datum:** a comparative study found KLab and Kontrol had "limited reliability even for small contracts."
- **Certora Prover / CVL**: **open-sourced under GPLv3 in 2025**, <https://github.com/Certora/CertoraProver>. 7+ years, >$100B TVL covered, **>70,000 rules written by developers**, now targeting EVM/Solana sBPF/Stellar WASM plus Move and Vyper.
- **Slither / Echidna / Foundry** (Trail of Bits / Crytic): actively maintained; Slither is a hard dependency of Echidna; Echidna has a **`foundry` mode** running `invariant_`-prefixed stateful invariants — the fuzzing ecosystems have converged.
- **Act, Scribble**: maintenance status **could not be confirmed. Do not assert either is alive.**

> **Supplies:** (a) the empirical lesson that **property-based fuzzing of invariants is the highest-yield-per-hour technique**, and that it works best when the tool is the one already used for tests; (b) a spec-language precedent (CVL) that is deliberately *not* the implementation language; (c) a warning that whole-VM symbolic execution does not reach reliability. **Why the semantics are inapplicable:** KEVM's semantic object is a transition on a single global `<accounts>`/`<storage>` cell indexed by a block context. **You cannot state a KEVM theorem without naming "the state"** — the exact thing Custos lacks globally.

### Move, the Move Prover, and Sui's owned-object fast path

**Lineage** [S]: created at Facebook for Libra/Diem (Sam Blackshear the original architect); Diem shut down 2022; team split into **Aptos Labs** and **Mysten Labs** (Sui), with the VM open-licensed. **Maturity mid-2026** [S]: Aptos institutional (SEC/CFTC joint guidance 17 Mar 2026 classifying APT as a digital commodity; ~$1.2B on-chain RWA); Sui leads consumer/DeFi (~$4.21B market cap, ~$552M DeFi TVL May 2026); **Movement Labs entered bankruptcy**, restructuring proposal due 13 Oct 2026.

**Move Prover (MVP)** [S]: Zhong, Cheang, Qadeer, Grieskamp, Blackshear, Park, Zohar, Barrett, Dill, "The Move Prover," CAV 2020, 137–150, <https://cs.stanford.edu/~yoniz/cav20.pdf>. Dill, Grieskamp, Park, Qadeer, Xu, Zhong, "Fast and Reliable Formal Verification of Smart Contracts with the Move Prover," TACAS 2022, arXiv:2110.08362 — three enabling transformations (alias-free memory model, fine-grained invariant checking, monomorphization) and the headline result: **"The entirety of the Diem blockchain's Move code was extensively specified and can be fully verified in a few minutes, and changes to the Diem framework must verify before being merged."**

**The stated limitation matters enormously to Custos** [S]: MVP has **not** been used to prove properties requiring reasoning about *sequences* of transactions; specifications are **local**, one execution of one function; **no built-in support for inductive invariants holding across a module's lifetime.**

> **Supplies:** (1) The **CI-gated, minutes-not-hours verification discipline** — "must verify before merge" is why Diem's framework was actually verified while most Solidity is not. This is the most transferable *practice* in the whole smart-contract world. (2) **Linear/affine resource types** as a model for governance capabilities that must not be duplicated or silently dropped (an authority delegation, a one-time-use enactment token). (3) **Why MVP's proof style is wrong for you:** your key theorems are exactly the inductive-invariants-over-a-sequence theorems MVP explicitly does not do. **Model your proof effort on Marlowe's Isabelle work, not MVP's per-function Hoare triples.**

**Sui Lutris — the strongest counterexample, handled carefully.** Blackshear, Chursin, Danezis, Kichidis, Kokoris-Kogias, Li, Logan, Menon, Nowacki, Sonnino, Williams, Zhang, **ACM CCS 2024**, doi:10.1145/3658644.3670286 · arXiv:2310.18042 · <https://docs.sui.io/paper/sui-lutris.pdf>

Abstract [V]: sub-second finality by "using consensusless agreement not only for simple payments but for a large variety of transactions," combined with a high-throughput consensus protocol; "latency lower than 0.5 seconds for throughput up to 5,000 certificates per second."

**⚠ The paper body could not be read** — PDF unparseable, arXiv HTML 404, ar5iv conversion failed, ACM 403. Paper-body claims are [S]. **Open the PDF yourself before quoting.**

[S] Paper-level claims: "Since owned objects are never under contention when the owner is honest, Sui Lutris validates transactions accessing only owned or read-only objects via the fast path." Shared objects "are only accessed through consensus to serialize access." Differentiator vs. prior consensusless work: a misconfigured client "risks deadlocking its account," judged unacceptable for production, so Sui Lutris confines damage such that **"client bugs only affect liveness of equivocated owned objects for a single epoch,"** with proofs — whereas prior systems "punish equivocation with permanent deadlock of the asset." First provably safe reconfiguration protocol for a consensusless blockchain. Live since May 2023.

[V] Confirmed from <https://docs.sui.io/concepts/object-ownership> — the ownership/versioning-path table: *Address-owned* → **Fastpath**; *Shared* → **Consensus**; ***Consensus-address owned / "party"*** → **Consensus despite single-address ownership**, recommended "when you want single-address ownership with consensus sequencing, for example to allow multiple inflight transactions against the same logical object **without fastpath locks**"; *Immutable* → fixed; *Wrapped* → depends on wrapper. Plus: "Shared-object access is *not* restricted by ownership, so secure it in Move with explicit authorization checks." **Note the "party object" type: Sui has since added an object kind that gives up the fast path in order to escape fastpath locking. That is an admission that the consensusless path's ergonomics are genuinely constraining.**

[V] From <https://docs.sui.io/concepts/sui-architecture/transaction-lifecycle>: "Certified effects guarantee transaction finality. After a full node observes certified effects, **Sui includes the transaction in a checkpoint** and never reverts it." End-to-end finality 400–700 ms.

The boundary, confirmed by follow-on work:
- **Stingray: Fast Concurrent Transactions Without Consensus**, arXiv:2501.06531 [V, abstract]. States the limitation precisely: existing systems "are unable to concurrently process (a) transactions accessing the same state, **even if they are (almost) commutative**, e.g., payments much smaller than an account's balance, and (b) **multi-party transactions, e.g., asset swaps.** Moreover, they are slow to recover from contention, requiring once-in-a-day synchronization." Adds a **replicated bounded counter** and **FastUnlock**.
- **Cuttlefish**, arXiv:2309.12715 [S]: "when conflicting transactions prevent certificate construction, the owned object becomes locked until the end of the epoch, so Sui restricts transactions to objects from a single owner, limiting fast-path applicability."

> **Honest verdict on Sui:** it *is* a genuine counterexample and the strongest one in production. But the qualifications all point the same way. The consensusless subset is exactly **single-writer state with no cross-object contention**; commutative-but-shared state and multi-party atomic operations both fall back to consensus; equivocation costs an epoch-scoped lock; and a total order is produced anyway via checkpoints. **This is not "consensus is optional." It is "consensus is the fallback, and the fast path is the ℐ-confluent subset."** Fully consistent with Kleppmann & Howard.

**FastPay** — Baudet, Danezis, Sonnino, AFT '20, 163–177, arXiv:2003.11506, code <https://github.com/novifinancial/fastpay> [S]. "The key design decision is **consensus-less operation**: FastPay is based on **Byzantine Consistent Broadcast** as its core primitive, foregoing the expenses of full atomic commit channels (consensus)." Sub-100 ms intra-continental confirmation; >80,000 tps with 20 authorities. Candid limitation: "FastPay exposes information about all transactions… as well as the timings." Successor **Zef** (WPES@CCS 2023) for privacy.

> **Why it works, in your vocabulary:** each account is a **single-writer log ordered by the owner's own sequence number.** No shared mutable state → no contention → agreement needed only on *validity*, never on order. **This is the same structure as a KERI KEL. FastPay is the cleanest existence proof that Custos's substrate can carry real economic semantics.**

**Linera** [S] — <https://linera.dev/protocol/overview.html>. Founded by **Mathieu Baudet** (FastPay co-author). Millions of parallel **microchains**; scale by adding chains, not blocks. "When microchains are operated by a single user, Linera uses a **simplified mempool-free consensus protocol inspired by reliable broadcast**." Chain taxonomy: personal/single-owner (owner proposes; validators guarantee "on each chain, at each height, there is at most one unique block"; **liveness relies on the owners**), temporary shared chains, public validator-managed chains. **Still pre-mainnet mid-2026** — Conway testnet since Dec 2025. *(Search caution: "Linea," the ConsenSys zkEVM, is a different project and pollutes results.)*

### DAO frameworks and on-chain governance DSLs [all S]

- **Aragon / aragonOS / Aragon Court.** **The Aragon Association announced dissolution 2 Nov 2023**, disbursing 86,343 ETH (~$155M) to ANT holders and burning ANT; reasons cited were "bureaucratic complexity, misaligned stakeholders, and failed attempts at modifying the governance." **Aragon Voice, Govern and Court were all retired as "too prescriptive and complex for broad adoption."** Aragon Court was a Schelling game (jurors vote for what they think other jurors will pick; off-consensus voters slashed). **Yet aragonOS v1 still powers Lido and Curve**, and Aragon continues on the modular Aragon OSx.
  > **The lesson, aimed straight at Custos: the sponsoring organisation died, the retired products were retired for being too prescriptive and complex, and the code outlived the org. An open standard must be designed to survive its sponsor and must resist the temptation to over-specify.**
- **Compound Governor Bravo / OpenZeppelin Governor.** The snapshot is *the* mechanism: "the snapshot is the timepoint used to retrieve a user's votes **and the quorum**," existing "to prevent double voting" by reading a past checkpoint — so "a voter must acquire tokens and delegate them before the snapshot is taken." `quorum(uint256 timepoint)`; `GovernorVotesQuorumFraction` typically 4%; with `GovernorCountingSimple` only For+Abstain count toward quorum. Clock mode standardised in **ERC-6372**; timestamps optional since v4.9 (uint48). `GovernorPreventLateQuorum` guards whale deadline-sniping. **This is the purest concentration of order-dependence in DAO governance; §4.4 item 5 dissects it.**
- **Moloch / Moloch v2.** ~300 lines vs The DAO's 1400+. **Ragequit**: members who disagree with a funding decision exit with their proportional treasury share *before the decision executes*. v2 adds multi-token treasuries, **Guild Kick**, non-voting **Loot** shares.
  > **Supplies: exit instead of agreement.** A minority that loses is made whole and leaves rather than being bound. In a "convergence, not consensus" system, **exit is the natural remedy for irreconcilable disagreement, and Moloch shows it can be a first-class mechanised primitive rather than a failure mode. This is the most philosophically aligned DAO primitive in existence.** Caveat: ragequit's accounting is a balance invariant and therefore non-ℐ-confluent.
- **DAOstack Holographic Consensus** — predictors stake on whether a proposal will pass; predictions boost/de-boost and *modify quorum requirements*. **Status unconfirmed; nothing found after 2019/2020.**
- **Colony** — reputation that **decays over time**, computed by **off-chain reputation mining**; asynchronous continuous decisions over a domain tree rather than synchronous referenda. Status unconfirmed. **Supplies:** decaying reputation is a *monotone-in-time* weight requiring no transferable token — closer to ℐ-confluent than token voting. But off-chain mining reintroduces a trusted computation.
- **Snapshot + SafeSnap + oSnap.** Votes are **cryptographic signatures, not transactions**, stored off-chain on IPFS; >35,000 spaces; >400 voting strategies; non-binding by default. **SafeSnap** (Gnosis Guild / Zodiac): each proposal becomes a **Reality.eth** question ("did this proposal pass, did it include this payload, does the payload do what the proposal describes"), bonded in the DAO's own token, 24-hour cooldown, then **anyone** can execute. **oSnap** (UMA + Snapshot Labs, audited by OpenZeppelin): bond + configurable liveness, unresolved disputes escalate to UMA's DVM.
  > **Why Snapshot is the most interesting DAO artifact for Custos:** it is *already* a system of signed statements with no consensus and no canonical state, working at enormous scale. **Its weakness is exactly what Custos solves: the tally is not self-certifying.** A Snapshot result is a claim by snapshot.org's indexer about a set of signatures plus a strategy evaluated at a block. **Frame Custos as "Snapshot with a verifiable tally" and you have an immediately legible pitch.** Note also that Snapshot's strategies still read on-chain balances at a snapshot block, so the *weight function* smuggles global state back in.
  > *(No evidence found for an "oz-relayer" Snapshot X integration; OpenZeppelin appears only as oSnap's auditor.)*
- **Arbitrum / Optimism Security Councils.** Arbitrum: 12 members, **9-of-12 can upgrade instantly with no delay**, 7-of-12 for slow upgrades bypassing DAO vote; all else through three timelocks; bound by the Arbitrum Constitution to emergency-only use plus transparency reports, elected in semi-annual cohorts, contractually bound to the Foundation. Optimism: appointed council, Safe multisig at 75%. Both DAOs retain authority to revoke council powers. Arbitrum's docs leave the endgame open: "How and when can the Security Council's power be further minimized, or eliminated entirely?"
- **Polkadot OpenGov.** **Origins/tracks**, each with its own dispatch privilege, parameters, and **Max Deciding** (max simultaneous referenda); four periods; **two distinct thresholds** — *approval* (conviction-weighted) and *support* (unconviction-weighted turnout vs total possible); approval curve decays from 100% toward but never below 50%; **conviction voting** = voluntary token lock as a power multiplier; multi-role delegation per track.
  > **Supplies: the *tracks* idea, which is excellent and substrate-independent** — different classes of amendment get different thresholds, delays and concurrency limits, all declared as data. **Custos should have tracks.** **Smuggles in:** conviction requires locking a transferable token for a wall-clock duration; Max Deciding is a FCFS concurrency limit; the approval curve is a function of elapsed time.
- **Tezos on-chain amendment.** Five periods (Proposal, Exploration, Cooldown, Promotion, Adoption), each 14 cycles (~14 days), ~2.5 months, automatically scheduled by the protocol. **Supermajority = Yea stake >80% of Yea+Nay; Pass counts neither way.** The protocol is downloaded, compiled and hot-swapped at a predetermined block height.
  > **The one genuinely important idea:** because the proposal/voting/activation process is *part of the economic protocol*, **the amendment rules are themselves amendable.** Custos needs exactly this recursive property. **Tezos is the only production system that treats it as a first-class design goal — study its answer to "which rules govern the amendment that changes the amendment rules."**
- **MakerDAO / Sky "spells."** A **spell** is a single `DssSpell` contract that atomically applies a batch of parameter changes. Four independent brakes, all governance-tunable: (1) **GSM Pause Delay** — enforced wait between passage and effect, moved 30h → 16h (Sept 2024) → 18h (18 Feb 2025, "as protection against potential governance attacks") → 48h (by 15 May 2025); (2) **office hours** — many spells executable only 14:00–21:00 UTC Mon–Fri; (3) **30-day expiry** — an unexecuted spell dies; (4) **Protego** (`MCD_PROTEGO`, added to the Chainlog 15 May 2025) — **cancels a queued spell during the pause delay.** Plus emergency standby spells.
  > **The single most directly borrowable governance pattern in this report.** A spell is exactly the right shape for a Custos governance event: **one signed, self-contained, atomically-applied bundle of parameter deltas, with a declared delay, a declared expiry, a cancellation window, and a pre-deployed emergency halt. None of the four brakes requires consensus** — they require a log position and a clock reference. **Copy the shape.**
- **Kleros.** Launched July 2018; jurors stake PNK to enter the draw; **court tree** with a root General Court and subject-matter subcourts; **anyone can appeal**, both sides funding the next round. Legal reception is live: whether awards issued "without seats or States" are enforceable under the New York Convention; Stanford Law has a socio-legal case study.
- **DAO legal wrappers.** **Wyoming DAO Supplement** (passed April 2021, eff. 1 July 2021; amended SF0068 May 2022, SF0075 Feb 2023): creates the "DAO LLC" with member limited liability, and — the key detail — **the articles must specify whether the entity is member-managed or *algorithmically* managed.** **Marshall Islands** DAO Act 2022 + 2024 regulations ("MIDAO" LLCs). **COALA DAO Model Law** as the reference non-statutory model. Catherine Martin Christopher, "Recognizing DAOs as Legal Entities," SSRN 5220994, 17 Apr 2025 — argues statutes must protect participants *and third parties* without being so onerous as to impede compliance, and should be consistent across jurisdictions.
  > **Supplies more than it looks.** Wyoming's "algorithmically managed" declaration is *a legal statement that the fold is the governance.* And Custos's core property — any stranger recomputes the same judgment from a file — **is precisely the third-party-protection property the Christopher paper says these statutes need. There is a real argument that a verifiable pure fold satisfies statutory transparency requirements better than an on-chain DAO does**, because an on-chain DAO's judgment is only recomputable by someone who can sync the chain. **Write that down; it is a differentiator.**

### Declarative and higher-level contract DSLs

**DAML / Canton (Digital Asset)** [S] — Daml came from Elevence (acquired 2016), open-sourced Apache 2.0 in April 2019.
**The partial-view model is real:** "in Daml's ledger model the ledger is **not fully replicated** among parties; it is segmented according to privacy rules, and each party stores only its view, or shard, of the ledger — so **there is no ledger view common to all parties**"; "**the global ledger is virtual.**" Each recipient obtains only the **subtransaction (projection)** it is entitled to see; other parts "are never shared with that participant, not even in encrypted form."
**Your specific question — does Canton require a total order per domain? YES, unambiguously.** "The domain must facilitate synchronization of the shared ledger by **establishing a total order of transactions**, doing so in an append-only fashion." The **sequencer** "orders all confirmation requests on a Canton domain, so that whenever two participants see the same two requests, they see them in the same sequencer order"; a **mediator** is the commit coordinator, and **"a contract must be locked before it can be consumed"** — two-phase commit with locking.
> **One genuinely borrowable idea:** "because the domain can only see encrypted transactions, its functional requirements are satisfied at a lower level than the Daml transaction level" — **the ordering service is semantics-blind.** That is exactly the role of a KERI witness: order/receipt without understanding. **Canton proves you can build a strong-consistency system where the ordering authority has zero semantic authority.**
Maturity [S]: "the institutional blockchain processing $4T+ annually"; reportedly seeking $300M at ~$2B valuation (Bloomberg, May 2026); partners DTCC, J.P. Morgan/Kinexys, HSBC, Visa, Mastercard, Franklin Templeton; Canton v3.4.

**Marlowe (IOHK/IOG → community)** [S] — Lamela Seijas & Thompson, ISoLA 2018; "Marlowe: Implementing and Analysing Financial Contracts on Blockchain," WTSC@FC 2020.
**The invariants are the point:** contracts are finite; **no recursion or loops**; **contracts will terminate**; **timeout on all actions**; contracts have a defined lifetime; no assets retained on close; **conservation of value**. The system is **formally verified in Isabelle**, proving "money in = money out." Because it is a DSL, "contracts can be exhaustively analysed without executing them" — SMT via the SBV monad. One executable Haskell semantics reused three ways (Haskell interpretation, Plutus compilation, PureScript browser simulation). Lineage: Peyton Jones/Eber/Seward composable contracts and LexiFi.
> **The mandatory timeout is the transferable idea and it is the answer to your clock problem.** Every waiting point has a continuation that fires on expiry, so **a participant can never deadlock the contract by refusing to act**, and the contract has a defined value at every point. That converts "wait until wall-clock T" into "this is a total function." **Custos should adopt mandatory-default-continuation on every predicate that waits.**
**Status:** `input-output-hk/marlowe-cardano` carries a notice that it "will soon be moved to <https://github.com/marlowe-lang>," administered by a not-for-profit being set up by a transition team led by **Simon Thompson**, Nicolas Henin, Tomasz Rybarczyk; README flags "pre-release software." **Alive but handed off from its corporate sponsor to a community vehicle.**

**Scilla (Zilliqa)** [S] — Sergey, Kumar, Hobor, arXiv:1801.00687; Coq embedding <https://github.com/ilyasergey/scilla-coq>. **"The underlying computation model in Scilla is based on communicating automata"**, giving "a clean separation between the communication aspect of smart contracts on a blockchain… and a programming component, which enjoys principled semantics and is amenable to formal verification." Explicitly an *intermediate* language. Safety *and* liveness proved for a Crowdfunding contract. **Honest limitation stated by the authors:** the model "does not feature resource semantics (i.e., Ethereum-style gas), full-fledged blockchain reflection, or advanced control-flow features such as continuations and exceptions."
> **Supplies:** the *separation of communication from computation* — exactly the separation Custos needs between "what events arrived" and "what the fold computes"; the explicit state-machine framing making the transition function a first-class artifact; and proof that a Coq-embedded contract-language semantics is achievable by a small team. **Not a source because** Scilla's automaton still transitions on blockchain-ordered messages, and the resource/termination question was left to the substrate.

**Pact (Kadena)** [S] — <https://github.com/kadena-io/pact>. "Turing-incomplete… there is no recursion (**recursion is detected before execution** and results in an error) and no ability to loop indefinitely"; `map`/`fold`/`filter` exist but "since **infinite lists cannot be defined**, these are necessarily bounded"; no variable reassignment. Kadena calls it a "spiritual successor to Bitcoin scripts." Property checking / `verify` shipped May 2018 with reusable named properties in a model block.
> **Supplies the specific enforcement technique: recursion detected statically before execution, and totality achieved by making unbounded structures unconstructible rather than by metering.** Also: Kadena's governance/upgradeability story is built into the module system rather than bolted on via proxies.

**Simplicity (Blockstream Research)** [S] — O'Connor, arXiv:1711.03028. **Nine primitive combinators**; typed, combinator-based, functional, **no loops or recursion**; "finitarily complete" but **Turing-incomplete**. **Formal denotational semantics in Coq**, plus operational semantics via the **Bit Machine** which **"measures the computational space and time resources needed to evaluate Simplicity programs"** — i.e. **the resource bound is part of the semantics, not a runtime meter.** Jets exist because low-level programs would otherwise be large and slow. **Deployed on Liquid only; Bitcoin activation would need a soft fork and no proposal has been made.**
> **The Bit Machine is the model to copy. A pure replayable fold has no meter and no payer, so the resource bound must be a static, derivable property of the program text.** Simplicity is the most rigorous demonstration that this is achievable with a formal semantics attached.

**Bitcoin Script / Miniscript / Policy — the most relevant prior art for predicates in this entire report** [S]. **BIP 379** is the spec: <https://bips.dev/379/> · reference compiler <https://miniscript.bitcoin.sipa.be/>.
**The three-layer architecture:** *Policy* is what you mean; *Miniscript* is its typed, analysable form; *Script* is the bytecode the network validates. Policy expresses **only** signature requirements, timelocks, hash preimages, and combinations: `multi(k, …)`, `after(n)` absolute timelock, `older(n)` relative timelock, plus AND/OR/threshold composition. "Unlike Simplicity, which can express any computable function, Miniscript is very limited in scope… **This reduction in scope makes Miniscript easier to reason about for the use cases it covers.**"
BIP 379 frames the problems it solves as *analysis obligations*: finding the most economical script for a set of conditions; **composing** two scripts (a multisig where one "key" is itself a multisig); **determining what conditions a script permits**; **constructing a satisfying witness given sufficient keys**; **predicting spending cost**; and **knowing whether resource limits like the ops limit might be hit**. Constraint: **keys may not be repeated**, to preserve non-malleability. Real deployments: Liana, Nunchuk vaults with timelock recovery branches by default.
> **This is the closest existing artifact to Custos's leaf-level predicate language. Treat BIP 379 as a design template.** Adopt: the **three-layer separation** (human policy / typed analysable IR / canonical serialised form in the log); the **analysis obligations as first-class language requirements** (satisfiability, witness construction, cost prediction, resource-limit checking); and the deliberately tiny expressive scope. **The one thing you cannot borrow is the clock** — `after(n)`/`older(n)` are enforced by Bitcoin consensus via nLockTime/nSequence. Everything else transfers essentially unchanged.

**Findel** [S] — Biryukov, Khovratovich, Tikhomirov, WTSC@FC 2017; Coq formalisations by Arusoaie (arXiv:1909.05464, arXiv:2005.13602). A contract is **a tree of primitives**; description and issuer immutable, ownership transferable; the `Or` primitive executes exactly one sub-node per an owner choice and deletes the other. **Honest limitation:** "Findel does not guarantee contracts progress properly — there is no default refund mechanism, and economic enforcement isn't handled by the language itself." **Exactly the failure Marlowe's mandatory timeouts fix — a good cautionary datapoint for why you need default continuations.**

**Obsidian (CMU)** [S] — Coblenz et al., TOPLAS 42(3) Art. 14 (2020), arXiv:1909.03523; usability study OOPSLA 2020, arXiv:2003.12209. **Typestate** to statically ensure objects are used correctly in their current state, plus **linear types** for assets; core calculus **Silica** with a type-soundness proof. Rationale: blockchain applications "typically implement state machines supporting different operations in different states" (→ typestate) and "frequently manipulate resources" (→ ownership). **The empirical result is the valuable part:** in a 20-participant study, **70% of Solidity participants accidentally inserted bugs that Obsidian's compiler would have caught** — but early typestate prototypes "caused significant user confusion," which is why Silica was deliberately simplified.
> **Supplies:** typestate is the right static discipline for a governance lifecycle (a proposal in `Draft` cannot be `Enact`ed), **and the usability finding is a warning that expressive type systems must be co-designed with users or they will not be used.**

**Nomos (CMU)** [S] — Das, Balzer, Hoffmann, Pfenning, Santurkar, arXiv:1902.06056; thesis CMU-CS-21-112. Three mechanisms for three problems: **shared binary session types** (rooted in linear logic) for interaction protocols, **automatic amortized resource analysis** for resource usage, **linear types** for assets. Soundness proof "ensures protocols are followed at runtime and that types give sound upper bounds on resource consumption — ruling out **re-entrancy** and **out-of-gas** vulnerabilities respectively." An **acquire-release discipline** rules out reentrancy.
> **The most important termination result for Custos: Nomos proves gas bounds can be a *typing* property rather than a runtime meter** — mandatory for a pure fold that strangers replay with no meter and no payer.

**Flint** — [R] a type-safe, capabilities-secure contract language from Imperial College (Schrans, Eisenbach, Drossopoulou) with typestate-like state annotations and asset types. **Not surfaced by search; do not cite without checking.** **Michelson / Liquidity** — related Coq work: "Making Tezos smart contracts more reliable with Coq" (Mi-Cho-Coq), arXiv:2106.12973.

## 4.3 Other genuinely non-globally-ordered work — ranked by usefulness

### Holochain — the closest existing thing to Custos, and a major lead [V + S]

**Architecture** [V, <https://developer.holochain.org/concepts/4_dht/>]: each agent keeps a personal **source chain**; "source chain *actions* are always public" even when entry data is private. Publishing produces **DHT operations** routed to three authority classes — **entry authorities**, **record authorities**, **agent activity authorities** (an author's complete history). Authorities check the signature "to make sure it hasn't been modified in transit," then run "system and app-level **validation rules**." Enforcement is by **warrant** — **"a signed proof that the author has broken a rule"** — published to agent activity authorities and gossiped; peers receiving a warrant block network communication with the warranted author: **"This is what creates Holochain's immune system."**

**Determinism, and how it is enforced** [S]: validation must "always yield the same true/false outcome for a given operation regardless of who is validating them and when." Crucially, **this is enforced structurally, not by convention: "Holochain prevents your validation callbacks from calling any non-deterministic host functions."** The **integrity zome / coordinator zome split** is the architectural expression — integrity zomes define entry/link types and the `validate` callback and depend only on `holochain_deterministic_integrity`; only coordinator zomes may use non-deterministic host functions. Dependencies "must be addressable, retrievable from the same DHT, and their addresses must be known or at least calculable."

**The `validate` callback** [S]: a single entry point taking an `Op`, returning `ValidateCallbackResult` — **`Valid` / `Invalid(String)` / `UnresolvedDependencies`**. Valid and Invalid are definitive ("the op won't be validated again"); `UnresolvedDependencies` fires automatically when a `must_get_*` call yields nothing, and tells Holochain to retry later. Called both by the author pre-publish and by validating peers. Because an operation encapsulates an *action*, **"the function is validating whether the action's author *should have performed it*,"** with access to the author's source chain and the DHT.

**Known determinism gotcha** [S]: with **private entries**, an author's validation runs with the entry present while a remote validator runs `validate` with the entry hidden — determinism is achievable but adds overhead, and developers may wrongly assume validators check private content.

**Maturity** [S]: 0.5 introduced the Kitsune2 networking engine; **0.6.x is the stable line for hApp developers while the core team develops 0.7**; **warrants were an "unstable feature" being stabilised for app and sys validation (except chain forks)**; DHT read-gating via membrane proofs was deferred out of 0.6 in favour of warrants. A 2025 community assessment was positive but sober: real velocity, but "every major application remained in alpha or development," full production readiness 1–2 years out.

> **Supplies four things, all directly adoptable:**
> 1. **A three-valued validation result.** `UnresolvedDependencies` is the missing primitive for a stranger-replayable fold over partial evidence: it makes the fold **total without forcing it to lie about insufficient evidence.** Custos should have this as a first-class outcome distinct from both valid and invalid, with a defined retry/re-fold discipline.
> 2. **Runtime-enforced purity via the integrity/coordinator split** — purity as a *sandbox property* rather than a coding convention. Custos's evaluator should structurally deny wall clock, network, local state, and iteration order over unordered collections.
> 3. **Warrants** — self-certifying, gossipable, **monotone** proof-of-rule-violation, with blocking as enforcement. A complete answer to "what do you do about a bad actor when you cannot revert their event," composing perfectly with KERI duplicity detection. **Adopt the concept and the name.**
> 4. **The framing that validation judges the author's authority to act**, not just the data's shape, with the author's own chain as context. That *is* a Custos governance predicate.
>
> **And a free warning:** the private-entry determinism gotcha is a bug class Custos will hit the moment it admits confidential governance data — which, per §1.7, ACDC's `u`-blinded rules already make possible.

### AT Protocol / Bluesky moderation — governance without consensus, in production, at scale [V + S]

**Label format** [V, <https://atproto.com/specs/label>]: fields are `ver`, **`src` (source DID)**, **`uri` (subject)**, **`val` (value, ≤128 bytes)**, plus optional `cid`, **`neg`** (negation), **`exp`** (expiration), **`sig`**. Signing mirrors repo commits: encode the label *excluding* `sig` in deterministic CBOR, SHA-256, sign with the key identified as `#atproto_label` in the issuer's DID document; "Signatures should be validated when labels are transferred between services." Recommended value syntax is lowercase kebab-case ASCII, with explicit advice **against** "packing additional structure in to value fields" (no encoded data, punctuation namespacing, URLs, whitespace, non-ASCII); `!`-prefixed values reserved for system behaviours. Distribution is two endpoints (`subscribeLabels` WebSocket with backfill; `queryLabels` filtered query). Labels need not be publicly enumerable. **Negation "retracts a previous label without implying the inverse is true."** Negated/expired labels should not be hydrated into API responses. **The spec contains no consensus or ordering requirement; labels are independent, self-authenticated objects.**

**Governance architecture** [S]: "there is no hardcoded consensus or moderation logic in the core protocol — community rules and byzantine voting mechanisms are built as higher-order applications subscribing to streams of relevant events." Moderation is externalised, with opinionated decisions "encapsulated as signed records." Services closer to the user are designed to be actively involved because they understand community norms; relays are "common infrastructure serving many kinds of applications." **The Bluesky app hardcodes its own labelling and stacks community labelers on top**, and a labeler built for Bluesky "could be reused by a hypothetical separate photo-sharing app." Consensus, where wanted, is layered *above*: PMsky publishes votes on labels as atproto records, "offering moderation by consensus rather than consolidated opinion."

> **The best real-world validation of Custos's thesis. Four gifts:**
> 1. **Composable/stackable judgment.** Multiple labelers publish *different, attributed* judgments over the same repos simultaneously, and the system does not break. That is "convergence, not consensus" at tens of millions of accounts. **Cite this as the existence proof that plural judgment is not chaos.**
> 2. **`neg` + `exp` as the revocation primitive** — retract without asserting the inverse; expire without deleting. The exact shape Custos needs for authority withdrawal, already specified, signed and deployed.
> 3. **Judgment is attributed to a `src` DID.** There is no "the label"; there is "the label *from this labeler*." Custos should likewise make every judgment carry its authority, so disagreement is legible rather than a fault.
> 4. **The deliberate flatness of `val`.** The warning against packing structure into the value field is a hard-won lesson about extensibility in a protocol with no central schema authority. **Heed it when designing Custos predicate identifiers.**

### Ceramic Network [S]
<https://developers.ceramic.network/protocol/streams/consensus/> — "Nodes can subscribe to subsets of streams, forgoing the need for a global network state"; **"global consensus and ordering aren't needed for progress, and most decisions are localized to the consuming party of a single event stream"**; **"if two parties consume the same set of events for a stream, they arrive at the same state"** — Custos's convergence claim, in a shipped protocol's own documentation. A log allows multiple parallel branches forming a tree; a valid log is one path from a tip back to the Init Event; multiple tips make canonical-tip selection "a consensus problem," resolved by traversing to a common Time Event or Init Event, ties broken by "the path with the greatest number of events from the last timestamp proof to the tip." **Pluggable per-StreamType conflict resolution:** the **earliest-anchor** rule (which *enables secure key revocation*, since a holder of a revoked key cannot produce an *earlier* proof-of-publication) versus the **latest-nonce** rule (which does not support key revocation). Anchor commits "contain a blockchain timestamp, providing an immutable record of time and ordering… sometimes called a **proof-of-publication**," needed "because vanilla merkle DAGs have no notion of absolute time." **The documented attack:** under earliest-anchor, "a stream controller can create anchored commits in secret and reveal them later," mitigated by requiring anchor services that publish all anchor commits as they are created. Ceramic One exposes both `stream_tips` (unresolved, "for developers building conflict resolution systems") and `stream_states` (post-resolution canonical).
> **Supplies:** (a) **the earliest-anchor trick for key revocation** — a genuinely clever partial answer to the revocation problem, since anchoring makes "who was authorised first" provable without consensus; (b) the documented attack against it **and its mitigation — build that in from the start**; (c) **the architectural pattern of exposing both unresolved tips and resolved state**, a good model for a Custos API that must serve both "the judgment" and "the evidence of disagreement."
> *(But see §5.13: Ceramic is deprecating js-ceramic and ComposeDB. The protocol documentation remains valuable; the platform should not be built on.)*

### Radix Cerberus — the right idea, not shipped as designed [S]
arXiv:2008.04450 (Core-, Optimistic-, Pessimistic-Cerberus). Cerberus **"departs from the traditional global ordering common to almost every DLT — where every transaction must be placed on the same timeline — and instead embraces partial ordering.** It uses a series of timelines that only ensure the ordering of related events." Independent per-shard HotStuff instances, cross-shard operations "braiding" only the shards a transaction touches; UTXO-like **substates**; 2^256 pre-sharded shard space. **The application layer must tell Cerberus which shards a transaction touches**, which is why Radix pairs it with a purpose-built engine — and the money quote: **"the EVM is designed around global ordering, since any transaction could change anything anywhere, making it fundamentally incompatible with Cerberus-style sharding."**
**Maturity caveat:** Jepsen's 2022 analysis (<https://jepsen.io/analyses/radix-dlt-1.0-beta.35.1>) found sharded Cerberus **not implemented**, with Radix running all transactions through a single HotStuff instance; Radix's own docs confirm Babylon mainnet uses a simplified unsharded Cerberus.
> **Supplies the strongest available third-party support for your cautionary thesis** — an explicit, quotable statement that **EVM semantics are inseparable from global ordering**, from a team that tried to build a partial-order platform and had to write a new execution layer to do it. **With the honest lesson that the hard part is the application layer, not the consensus layer.**

### Nano and IOTA — instructive contrast [S]
**Nano**: block lattice, each account its own chain, asynchronous send/receive split so transactions process in parallel. **"Because only account holders can append to their own chain, all forks must result from poor programming or malicious intent (double-spend) by the account owner, which means nodes can easily make policy decisions on how to handle them."** **But** resolution still requires **ORV** — balance-weighted representative voting — i.e. consensus by another name; a survey notes the scheme "requires high node synchrony and may not scale well with network size" (arXiv:1904.04098).
**IOTA is the cautionary tale**: the Coordinator existed for years precisely because the partial-order Tangle could not prevent double-spends alone; "Coordicide" concluded not by securing the partial order but by **abandoning it** — the 2025 **Rebased** upgrade removed the Coordinator by moving to a DPoS validator committee (up to 100 validators), Move smart contracts, and transaction fees with burning.
> **Lesson: a partial-order DAG by itself does not solve non-ℐ-confluent invariants.** Nano gets away with less because it partitions to single-writer accounts; IOTA tried to have a partial order *and* a shared-scarcity invariant and had to introduce agreement. **This is Kleppmann & Howard's theorem showing up as a decade of engineering history.**

### Farcaster [S]
<https://github.com/farcasterxyz/protocol/blob/main/docs/SPECIFICATION.md> — CRDT-based sets with per-message eviction. **Tie-break by (timestamp, hash)**, so an otherwise-ambiguous choice becomes a pure function; because deltas are totally ordered this way, size-based eviction **"does not change the CRDT's guarantees."** **Supplies:** the cheap engineering answer to deterministic tie-breaking, and the proof that **bounded, evicting CRDTs stay convergent under a deterministic total order.**

### Secure Scuttlebutt and Hypercore [S]
**SSB**: Tarr, Lavoie, Meyer, Tschudin, "Secure Scuttlebutt: An Identity-Centric Protocol for **Subjective** and Decentralized Applications," ICN '19, doi:10.1145/3357150.3357396. Integrity requires more than signature checks: "sequence numbers must be in order, backlinks must correctly reference the previous item, and the feed must not be forked." **"How nodes should react to forked feeds was left as open future work."** The deployed **transitive-interest model** "scales to thousands of participants and is spam- and sybil-resistant."
> **Supplies:** the word **"subjective"** as the honest name for judgment-from-held-evidence; the insight that **replication topology is itself a Sybil defense and therefore a governance choice**; and the datapoint that its open problem (fork response) is *your* problem — **which is a useful signal that KERI duplicity handling is a genuine contribution rather than a detail.**
**Hypercore** (formerly Dat, renamed May 2020): DEP-0002 (Hypercore) and DEP-0010 (wire protocol) at <https://www.datprotocol.com/deps/>. Signed append-only log identified by a public key, Merkle-verified, with a transport-agnostic wire protocol that syncs "sparsely and live." **Supplies the engineering:** sparse, live, verifiable partial replication of an append-only log is exactly what a Custos watcher needs, and **DEP-0010 is a good reference design.**

### Partial verdicts and two negative findings
- **DAML/Canton:** partial views yes, per-domain total order **yes**. Not a consistency counterexample.
- **Optimistic / zk rollups:** validity is locally checkable, but rollup *state* is a single sequenced chain and the dispute game resolves *to* a canonical order. The transferable part is the challenge game (§4.7), not the consistency model.
- **Avalanche:** [R] its DAG converges to a total order; not a counterexample. Not investigated.
- **"Chris Meiklejohn critique of blockchain consistency" — no such essay found.** His distributed-systems hub covers coordination-free programming, Lasp/Partisan/SyncFree, and CRDTs; a March 2026 post invokes CRDTs as merging correctly "regardless of update order or network partitions — with no consensus protocol, leader election, or locking." **Likely name confusion in the original brief: blockchain-consensus systematisation is *Sarah* Meiklejohn** — Bano, Sonnino, Al-Bassam, Azouvi, McCorry, S. Meiklejohn, Danezis, "Consensus in the Age of Blockchains," arXiv:1711.03936. **For the coordination-avoidance canon, cite Bailis.**

## 4.4 What exactly breaks — mechanism by mechanism

**1. Nonces / replay protection — ORDER-SENSITIVE, and a uniqueness constraint.** A transaction is valid iff `tx.nonce == account.nonce`; the nonce is not merely a replay guard, it is a *sequencer*. Two transactions with nonce *n* are each individually valid; only the total order rejects the second — textbook non-ℐ-confluent.
*What survives:* replace "the counter must equal current state" with **"the event must name its unique predecessor by digest."** Two events at the same sequence number then become **cryptographic proof of duplicity** rather than silent state corruption. This is what KERI already does and it is strictly better than a nonce: the fault is self-evidencing and attributable. **Do not port nonces; port the hash chain and make forks a first-class detectable fault.** Compare Nano's framing [S]: because only the account holder can append, all forks are the owner's fault, so "nodes can easily make policy decisions on how to handle them."

**2. `msg.sender` — FINE. Reentrancy — CATEGORY ERROR.** `msg.sender` maps cleanly onto a KERI AID, and arguably better (a signature over an event is stronger evidence than a call frame). Reentrancy is an artifact of *synchronous nested calls observing partially-mutated shared state mid-invocation*. **A pure fold has no call stack and no externally-observable intermediate state, so reentrancy does not exist as a bug class. Do not import checks-effects-interactions, reentrancy guards, or mutex ceremonies** — they solve a problem you don't have and will mislead readers about your execution model. (Nomos's acquire-release discipline is the principled version if you ever add shared session-typed interaction.)

**3. Global mutable storage — ORDER-SENSITIVE, and the root cause of almost everything else.** Solidity is a state transformer over a single global key-value store; every read means "the value at the moment my transaction executes," requiring a total order over all writers.
*What survives:* within one governance log the fold's state is derived and the order comes from the log's own hash chain — **single-domain parameter state is fine.** What breaks is *shared* mutable state written by multiple independent logs. For that you need either (a) commutative/associative/idempotent merge, or (b) partition into single-writer shares. **Sui owned objects, FastPay per-account sequence numbers, Linera single-owner chains and Stingray bounded counters are all the same trick: make every mutable cell have exactly one writer.** That trick is the whole reason those systems can skip consensus.

**4. `block.timestamp` / `block.number` as a clock — ORDER-SENSITIVE *and* AGREEMENT-SENSITIVE. The hardest one.** Governance leans on the clock constantly: OZ Governor's `votingDelay`/`votingPeriod` in ERC-6372 units; Maker's pause delay and office hours; Polkadot's four periods and conviction locks; Tezos's 14-cycle periods; Arbitrum's 7-day challenge window; Miniscript's `after(n)`/`older(n)`.
*What survives, strongest first:*
- **Log position as a relative clock** — "at least N events after event D." Fully self-certifying. Use wherever a delay is about *procedure* rather than wall time.
- **Witnessed / anchored time** — witness receipt timestamps, or anchoring digests into an external time source. **Ceramic does exactly this** and documents the attack you inherit (secret anchoring, late reveal) plus the mitigation (require anchor services that publish all anchors as created). **Build the mitigation in from the start.**
- **Marlowe-style mandatory timeout with default continuation** — every waiting predicate declares what happens if nothing arrives, so the fold is total at every point and **no participant can deadlock by inaction.**
*What does not survive:* any rule of the form "quorum must be reached *by* time T" where T is wall-clock and the tally is over concurrent, independently-authored events. Restate as **"quorum is reached at the event that first makes the count ≥ threshold, and that event's position is the deadline reference."**

**5. Quorum / threshold over a vote snapshot — the most concentrated order-dependence in DAO governance. Splits sharply.** OZ Governor needs three things at a timepoint: `getPastVotes(voter, t)`, `quorum(t)` (often a fraction of `totalSupply` at t), and no-double-voting. Decompose:
- **Counting distinct signatures toward `≥ k` over a fixed, enumerable roster — ℐ-confluent and monotone.** More evidence only moves you from "not yet" to "satisfied," never back. Well-defined with **zero ordering.** **This is exactly what KERI already does natively with `kt`/`k` and fractionally-weighted thresholds. This is your governance primitive, and it is strictly stronger than a token vote because it is self-certifying.**
- **Weights transferable between voters (a token) — non-ℐ-confluent, full stop**, by the balance argument. **Token-weighted governance cannot be made safe without consensus. Do not try.**
- **Quorum as a fraction of a changing denominator** — requires a global state read. Replace with an absolute threshold, or a fraction of a roster fixed by a prior event digest.
- **Vote revocation / re-voting — non-monotone**, therefore order-sensitive. Either forbid it, or define it via digest-named supersession.
- **"Quorum first reached at block N"** — order-sensitive, because "first" is a uniqueness claim. Restate as "the event that makes the count ≥ k," identified by digest, and accept that two observers with different evidence may name different events until they reconcile.
> *The free upgrade:* **replace time-keyed snapshots with digest-keyed snapshots.** "The roster as of event D" is content-addressed, self-certifying, and needs no clock, no block number, and no ERC-6372. **This is a strict improvement over the Governor design and worth stating as such.**

**6. Balance checks / double-spend / any budget — non-ℐ-confluent. The hard wall.** The theorem is explicit [V]: "payment transactions from the same payer are not ℐ-confluent with regard to the account balance invariant." **Every governance rule with a *budget* inherits this:** treasury spending caps, "at most N grants per period," rate limits, staking, quadratic funding, Moloch's ragequit accounting.
*The only two escapes:* (a) **partition the resource into single-writer shares** — give each sub-authority its own budget in its own log, so each share is decremented by exactly one author and each share's invariant is ℐ-confluent (this is Sui/FastPay/Linera); (b) **bounded counters** with pre-allocated per-replica allowances (Stingray's answer for the "almost commutative" case).
> **Write this into the Custos spec as a design rule: any scarce resource that must be governed without consensus must first be partitioned into single-writer shares.**

**7. `require` failure atomicity and revert semantics — a SEMANTICS OF FAILURE that must be redesigned, not ported.** The EVM's all-or-nothing revert presumes a transaction boundary inside a serialised execution *and the ability to un-happen*. **An append-only log cannot revert.** An event that fails validation was still committed.
*The redesign:* the fold must be a **total function on all syntactically well-formed events**, mapping an invalid event to *no state change plus a durable fault record*. **Holochain's three-valued result is the model** — `Valid` / `Invalid(String)` / `UnresolvedDependencies`, where the third means "required dependencies couldn't be fetched" and instructs a retry. **That third value is the key insight for a stranger-replayable fold and Custos should have it: it distinguishes "this is wrong" from "my evidence is insufficient," which is precisely the distinction a partial-evidence evaluator needs in order to be total without lying.**
*Cross-log atomicity is genuinely lost.* Canton buys it with a per-domain sequencer plus mediator 2PC with contract locking. Stingray states plainly that multi-party operations like asset swaps are what consensusless paths cannot do. **So: offer *eventual* cross-domain coordination — each domain records its own commitment, the joint effect observable only to someone holding both logs — and say so explicitly rather than implying atomicity.**

**8. Gas metering as a termination bound — DOES NOT TRANSFER AT ALL. Must become a type-system property.** Gas works because every validator executes the same code in the same order and charges the same fee to a payer. **In a pure fold replayed by arbitrary strangers there is no meter and no payer.** Termination must be intrinsic and statically derivable from the program text. The evidence from every DSL designed for third-party replay is unanimous — Pact (recursion detected before execution; infinite lists unconstructible), Simplicity (nine combinators, Bit Machine static bounds), Marlowe (finite, timeout on all actions, guaranteed termination), Miniscript (ops-limit checking as a language obligation), Nomos (static bounds by typing), and Scilla's authors explicitly excluding resource semantics from their model.
> **Answer to your question: yes, the governance predicate language must be total and terminating, and the bound must be statically derivable.** Miniscript is the closest prior art for the predicate layer; Nomos for "the type system proves the bound"; Simplicity for "the resource model is part of the formal semantics."

**9. Sequential composition / atomic multi-call — ACTUALLY EASY, and Maker found the right shape.** Governor's `execute(targets[], values[], calldatas[])` presumes atomic sequenced application. **But within a single event, ordering is textual, not temporal.** A governance event carrying a *list* of amendments applies them in the order written, deterministically, with **no coordination whatsoever**. **Maker's `DssSpell` is exactly this**, and Protego's "cancel a queued spell during the pause delay" maps onto "a later event within the delay window nullifies the pending one" — order-sensitive but *intra-log*, hence free. **Copy the spell shape.** What you cannot do is atomically compose across *logs*.

**10. Upgrade proxies — the machinery is unnecessary, but it hides a question you MUST answer.** `delegatecall` proxies, EIP-1967/1822 slots, UUPS, storage-layout discipline — all exist because deployed code is immutable *and* lives at a fixed address in a global namespace. In a fold, "the code" is the rule set derived from the log, and amendment is another event. Upgradeability is free and the Parity bug class evaporates.
> **But the risk inverts into a question blockchains never had to answer: does an amendment apply retroactively to the log's prefix, or only prospectively?** An EVM contract has only "current code + current state," so the question cannot arise. A fold over a whole log *can* be recomputed under new rules — **that is retroactive legislation. Custos must specify this explicitly.** Recommendation: **law-in-force-at-event-position** — the fold applies to each event the rule version in force at that event's log position. Ordinary legal practice (non-retroactivity), keeps the fold pure, and makes historical judgments stable under future amendment. Tezos's self-amendment is the nearest analogue and has the same structural question, including the recursive case. *(Note this converges with §5.14(b) and with LegalRuleML's repealed/annulled/suspended taxonomy in §5.11.)*

**11. Oracle reads — improve, don't port.** `oracle.latestAnswer()` presumes one canonical external fact at one point in one order. **In a log world the native form is a signed attestation from a named attestor**, so the predicate becomes "≥ k attestations from roster R asserting X" — **monotone and ℐ-confluent, i.e. better behaved than the EVM form.** The escalation designs are directly implementable: **Reality.eth/SafeSnap** and **oSnap** both reduce to *assertion event + bond + challenge window + named fallback arbiter*, where the window is measured in log positions or anchored time and the arbiter is a roster of AIDs. **99.8% of UMA requests resolve on the optimistic path without escalation — design for the uncontested case.**

**12. First-come-first-served — ORDER-SENSITIVE, and this is a policy problem, not an encoding problem.** Auctions, allowlists, mint caps, "the first 100," Polkadot's Max Deciding, "only one live executive spell." All are uniqueness/priority claims, forbidden without consensus.
*Options:* (a) designate a **single writer/sequencer** for the contended resource; (b) restate as a **monotone** rule ("all proposals reaching quorum take effect") plus a **deterministic tie-break** (Farcaster's `(timestamp, hash)`).
> **Be explicit with users that (b) changes the policy, not merely its encoding. Silently converting "first past the post" into "all who qualify" is a governance change disguised as a refactor, and that is the kind of thing that erodes trust in a standard.**

**13. Revocation and authority withdrawal — the genuinely open problem. Do not pretend otherwise.** "X had authority when the event was signed" versus "X's authority was revoked concurrently" cannot be resolved from causal history alone, because the two events are concurrent by construction. Kleppmann's PaPoC 2025 keynote frames this as **open research** [V].
*Partial mitigations:* AT Protocol's **`neg` + `exp`** gives attributed, non-destructive withdrawal [V]; Holochain's **warrants** are monotone fault evidence — once you hold a warrant it never becomes un-held [V]; Ceramic's **earliest-anchor** rule makes "who was authorised first" provable without consensus [S]; and see §5.5 for the 2025–26 literature.
> **Recommendation: state revocation semantics as an explicit, named limitation of Custos v1 with a documented conservative default** — e.g. authority is evaluated against the causal past of the event *as named by digest*, and later-discovered revocation produces a fault record rather than retroactively invalidating downstream state. **Do not ship an under-specified answer here.**

## 4.5 The taxonomy: order-sensitive vs state-sensitive

**ORDER-SENSITIVE** — needs a total order over *concurrent* events; cannot be fixed by naming a prefix:
nonces; FCFS/auctions/allowlists/caps; "at most one X"; budget decrements by multiple writers; last-write-wins parameter sets from concurrent authors; vote revocation and re-voting; "first to reach quorum"; revoke-then-act vs act-then-revoke; cross-log atomic composition; concurrency limits like Max Deciding.

**STATE-SENSITIVE but NOT order-sensitive** — fully repaired by digest-naming the prefix:
roster membership; weights; thresholds; parameter values; delegation graphs; vote snapshots (once keyed by digest rather than time); "the law in force at position P."

**ALREADY WELL-DEFINED** — monotone and/or commutative, no ordering needed:
counting distinct signatures toward `≥ k`; set-union of endorsements/attestations; disjunction of predicates; timelocks as "≥ N events after D"; attestation thresholds; **accumulation of fault evidence (duplicity proofs, warrants) — monotone by construction, which is exactly why they work.**

**BECOMES WELL-DEFINED IF MADE MONOTONE OR CRDT-LIKE:**
quorum, if `≥` with no expiry or retraction; parameter merge, with a deterministic `(timestamp, hash)` tie-break; eviction/bounded state, if the ordering is total and deterministic; budgets, if partitioned per-writer or expressed as bounded counters.

**BECOMES WELL-DEFINED IF THE RULE READS ONLY A CAUSALLY-CLOSED, DIGEST-NAMED PREFIX:**
essentially every state-sensitive rule above.
> **This is the single highest-leverage design constraint in Custos. Make "reads only a digest-named causally-closed prefix" a *type-level* property of predicates, checkable by the compiler**, in the way Holochain's integrity zomes structurally cannot call non-deterministic host functions. **Convention will not hold; the language must enforce it.**

## 4.6 Formal verification: what actually worked

Ranked by demonstrated payoff [all S unless noted]:
1. **Design-for-verification plus CI gating.** Move + Move Prover: the entire Diem framework specified and verifiable **in minutes**, and **"changes to the Diem framework must verify before being merged."** The enabling factors were architectural — a language designed for verification, a "culture of specification" from day one — not tooling heroics. **This is the practice to copy: verification that runs in minutes and blocks merges.**
2. **Narrow DSLs where exhaustive analysis is possible without execution.** Marlowe's SMT analysis plus Isabelle proofs of conservation of value; Miniscript's static cost/witness/ops-limit/satisfiability analysis as a language obligation; Pact's built-in `verify`. **The most relevant model for Custos, because a governance predicate language *is* a narrow DSL.**
3. **Property-based fuzzing of invariants, integrated with the normal test tool.** Echidna's `foundry` mode; Slither as a required preprocessor. Cheap, empirically effective, and the convergence with Foundry is why it gets used.
4. **Commercial FV with a purpose-built spec language.** Certora CVL: >70,000 rules, >$100B TVL, now GPLv3.

**What worked less well:** whole-VM symbolic execution (KLab/Kontrol at "limited reliability even for small contracts"); research-grade proof-assistant embeddings that stayed research-grade.

> **The mismatch to fix:** Move Prover's specifications are *local* — one execution of one function — with no support for inductive invariants across a module's lifetime. **A fold's central theorems are exactly inductive invariants over event sequences. Model your proof effort on Marlowe's Isabelle work (conservation of value = a fold invariant), not on per-function Hoare triples.**

## 4.7 Upgradeability vs immutability regret, and the oracle problem

**The three-act arc, worth reproducing in the Custos rationale** [all S]:
1. **The DAO (2016).** >$150M raised, >$60M drained via a vulnerability the code permitted; the attacker publicly claimed to have "rightfully claimed 3,641,694 ether" as a reward for using an intentional feature. Resolution: a contentious hard fork rolling back to pre-attack state. **Cost: permanent schism (ETH/ETC).** The philosophical point critics make: once someone had to choose which chain to run, **human choice determined which code is law.**
2. **Parity (Nov 2017).** `devops199` initialised the library contract itself as a wallet, claimed owner rights, and `selfdestruct`ed it, bricking dependent wallets holding **513,774.16 ETH** — including ~$90M from the Polkadot sale. A contributor had warned about the flaw in August 2017; Parity classified it as a "convenience enhancement," not an exploitable bug. **No fork happened. The funds have never been recovered.** *(Wallet counts vary across sources — 151 / 573 / 587 — but 513,774.16 ETH is consistent.)*
3. **L2 Security Councils (2023– ).** Intervention **pre-authorised**, with named holders, bounded scope, mandatory delays for the non-emergency path, transparency obligations, periodic re-election, and a retained DAO power to revoke.

> **Lessons:**
> - **Put the emergency power in the law, with named holders, bounded scope, mandatory delay, transparency obligations, and periodic re-election.** The alternative is not purity; the alternative is either a schism or half a million ETH frozen forever.
> - **Note the asymmetry the L2 designs settled on:** councils may change *future* rules but not rewrite settled history. **In an append-only log this asymmetry is structural, not a policy choice** — history-rewriting is simply not available. **That is a genuine advantage of Custos's substrate and you should say so.**
> - **Maker's four brakes are the tunable, borrowable version** — and none requires consensus.
> - **Aragon's retreat is the anti-pattern.** Design the standard to survive its sponsor, and resist prescriptiveness.

**The oracle problem — four designs, four lessons** [S]. **Reality.eth/SafeSnap**: bonded question, 24-hour cooldown, then anyone may execute. **oSnap / UMA Optimistic Oracle**: bonded assertions accepted automatically if undisputed within a 2h–2d liveness window; disputes escalate to the DVM (24h commit + 24h reveal, ≥65% of staked UMA); security argument is that corrupting the oracle costs more than the profit from doing so; an **Escalation Manager** contract makes the escalation policy pluggable; **99.8% resolve on the optimistic path**; and a Nov 2025 change restricted *proposal* rights to 37 pre-approved addresses **while keeping *disputes* open to anyone.** **Kleros**: court tree, staked draw, appeals funded by both sides, commit-reveal. **Augur**: escalating dispute bonds until a threshold, then an **algorithmic fork creating a child universe per outcome**; REP holders must actively choose; up to 60 days; "market resolution method of last resort."
> **Lessons: (1) design for the uncontested path; (2) bond + challenge window converts "compute the truth globally" into "assert locally, anyone may object"; (3) the ultimate backstop should not be more voting.** **Augur's fork is the purest, and it is Custos's own philosophy stated from inside the blockchain world: when agreement is impossible, split the universe and let value follow.** Also note UMA's asymmetry — **permissioned proposers, permissionless disputers** — which is well-suited to Custos: authoring is authorised, challenging is universal.

## 4.8 Challenge games — the most under-appreciated transferable idea in this report

**Arbitrum** [S] — Kalodner, Goldfeder, Chen, Weinberg, Felten, "Arbitrum: Scalable, private smart contracts," **27th USENIX Security Symposium, 2018**, <https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-kalodner.pdf>. Verifiers "employ a bisection protocol to narrow the disagreement down to the execution of a single instruction, and then one manager submits a simple proof of that one-instruction execution which the verifiers can check very efficiently." Soundness is symmetric: if every bisection is truthful the challenger's path leads to a correct one-step proof; if any half is false the challenger can always pick the false half. Modern Nitro/BoLD is multi-level: global states → a block → chunks of 2^26 WASM instructions → a single instruction → `oneStepProveExecution`, inside a 7-day window. **The crucial property: "the parent chain referee only checks that moves have the right shape (e.g., that the bisection was performed correctly) without evaluating execution correctness."**

**TrueBit** [S] — Teutsch & Reitwießner, 16 Nov 2017, <https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~teutsch/papers/truebit.pdf>; arXiv:1908.04756. An incentive layer over a dispute layer taking the form of a "verification game" between a Solver and a Challenger, refereed by "Judges" who "always perform computations correctly but have extremely limited computational bandwidth." Merkle roots of full state at each step let a 10^6-step dispute narrow to one step in ~20 rounds. **A single honest, uncensored actor prevents any attack** — turning a 51% requirement into 100%. Lineage: Canetti, Riva & Rothblum's refereed delegation (2011). **Known limits:** the game is inefficient so security degrades with computation complexity and data size; **not Sybil-proof**; and **unavailability of program data breaks the game.**

> **Why this matters enormously to Custos.** A challenge game replaces "compute the truth globally" with **"anyone may challenge, and a bounded interactive protocol resolves it, with a referee who checks only the shape of moves."** Custos has no on-chain referee — but it has something better: **a pure, deterministic fold whose every step is individually checkable.** So you can define a **judgment dispute protocol**: a party publishes a claimed fold result as a digest; a disagreeing party bisects the disagreement over the log by digest; they converge in **O(log n) rounds** on a single event application; the loser's error is exhibited as a **one-step proof any stranger checks in O(1)** — without anyone recomputing the whole log. **This makes disagreement about judgment cheap to adjudicate, which is exactly what a system with plural evidence bases needs. Treat it as a first-class Custos feature, not an optional extra.** *(Note it composes with §5.1's proof-carrying CRDTs: the succinct-proof profile and the bisection profile are complementary answers to "verify without replaying.")*
>
> **And inherit TrueBit's warning: challenge games have a hard data-availability precondition.** If the disputed evidence cannot be retrieved, the game breaks. In Custos that precondition is discharged by KERI witnesses and watchers — so **the availability duty is not incidental infrastructure, it is a soundness requirement of the dispute protocol. Say so explicitly.**

## 4.9 Termination bounds compared

## 5.0 The two headlines

1. **There is a live, named, five-year research programme that is almost exactly Custos's problem**, and it is not the one you would expect (not Ink & Switch, not UCAN): **Florian Jacob & Hannes Hartenstein's group at KIT (DSN/KASTEL)**, whose 2025 paper is titled *"To the Best of Knowledge and Belief: On Eventually Consistent Access Control."* They have formalised (a) partial-order-native access control as a data type, (b) the poset algebra under it, (c) succinct proofs so a stranger need not hold the whole log, and (d) machine-checked verification of the result.

**"To the Best of Knowledge and Belief" (CODASPY '25)** [S] contrasts *linearizable access control* (LAC) — the trusted-central-entity convention — with systems that cannot afford inter-entity dependencies, arguing LAC's strength comes *from* those dependencies "at the cost of availability, scalability, and resilience under faults." Its core construct is **"access control decisions that are correct to the best of knowledge and belief locally available to the deciding entity."**

> **Supplies: a defensible correctness *definition* for observer-local judgment.** "Every stranger recomputes the same byte" is only achievable if all strangers hold the same evidence. This paper gives you peer-reviewed language for what correctness means when they don't — which is the thing Custos most needs and cannot get from the legal literature. Open copy: <https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000176494/156112669>.

**Extend-Only Directed Posets (EDP)** [S, arXiv:2304.04318] unifies DAG-based Byzantine CRDT designs into one structure: replica state is a **monotonically growing partially ordered set, converged by set union as the join**, so "Byzantine tolerance arises from monotonic growth and deterministic merging rather than from coordination or consensus." Derives an EDP key-value map and sketches "an EDP-based systemic access control CRDT as formalization of the CRDT used in the Matrix messaging system."

> **Supplies: the algebra for your fold, and the single most reusable formal object found.** A KERI KEL — and a set of KELs/TELs — *is* an extend-only directed poset. If the Custos fold is shown to be a **monotone function from an EDP into a lattice**, then "any stranger recomputes the same judgment" stops being an implementation discipline and becomes a theorem.

**Proof-Carrying CRDTs (PaPoC '25)** [S] — motivation: in Byzantine settings an update's validity may depend on all previous updates, so processes "typically must retain a grow-only set of all applied updates"; proof-carrying data means "processes neither need knowledge of all previous updates nor coordination." Later work summarises it as attaching "succinct cryptographic proofs to each update attesting that the update **and its causal history satisfy a predefined validity predicate**." Evaluated on an increment-only counter and a Matrix-based update-history CRDT. Code: <https://github.com/kit-dsn/proof-carrying-crdts> (TypeScript, MIT).

> **Supplies: the answer to Custos's inevitable scaling objection.** "Predicates over a committed log" implies unbounded verification cost. This shows the predicate-over-causal-history can be compiled into a recursive proof and shipped as a constant-size witness — **a stranger verifies the judgment without replaying the log at all.** That is a *stronger* claim than byte-identical recomputation and probably belongs in Custos as an optional profile. Note the phrase "validity predicate over an update and its causal history" is, verbatim, your problem statement.

**Formal verification in Verus (arXiv:2604.23560, Apr 2026)** [V, abstract fetched], verbatim highlights: *"Matrix and Keyhive pair an informal specification with an unverified reference implementation… we contribute semantics and invariants of a replicated data type for managing simplified collaboration groups, based on **capabilities for access control and hash chronicles for replication**… using the Rust programming language for formal specification, verification, and implementation, enabled by the **Verus framework using the Z3 theorem prover at zero runtime cost**."*

> **Supplies:** an existence proof that this class of fold can be machine-checked *in the implementation language*, which is what you need if "byte for byte" is normative. Plus a blunt competitive observation: **the two flagship real systems in this space are informally specified.** Custos can differentiate by not being.

**Status: alive and productive** — 2026 preprint, active group page. Contact: Florian Jacob, <https://discuss.systems/@florianjacob>. He has a stated interest in scaling the verified model to real-world local-first access control systems.

## 5.2 Matrix state resolution — the only large-scale *deployed* partial-order-native governance system

Matrix rooms resolve membership, bans, join rules and power levels over a DAG of signed events with **no consensus and no finality**; every server computes room state as a deterministic function of the events it holds.

Key facts [S]:
- The controlling constraint is **partial replication**: "state resolution needs to work even if a server doesn't have a copy of the whole room DAG" — hence a deterministic function rather than a consensus algorithm. **That is Custos's exact constraint.**
- The algorithm **separates "control events" (power levels, membership, join rules) from ordinary state events**, computes a conflicted set plus an **"auth difference,"** reverse-topologically orders control events via an adaptation of **Kahn's algorithm** over the auth-event DAG, performs **iterative auth checks**, then sorts remaining state by the derived power levels.
- Failure modes that motivated v2 are the ones Custos will hit: **state resets** (a delayed event pointing at an old part of the DAG reverts current law); **moderation evasion by forking the DAG to escape a ban**; reliance on the untrustable self-asserted `depth` field; and the "Alice grants Bob, Bob grants Charlie on a fork" auth-chain problem.
- Still evolving in production: **room version 12** changes iterative auth checks to start from an *empty* state map rather than the unconflicted one, and adds a "conflicted state subgraph," via **MSC4297 "State Resolution v2.1"**, motivated by real observed state resets on the public federation. A 2025 "Project Hydra" effort targets state-res performance.

> **Supplies: a decade of adversarial operational experience with precisely "judgment computed from a partial order of governance events," plus a catalogue of named attacks.** Two transferable design rules: **(i) stratify your predicates — resolve authority-conferring events before authority-consuming ones**; **(ii) never let a wall-clock or a self-asserted depth field into the fold.** And one cautionary datapoint: state resolution has needed **three algorithm revisions in production**, so Custos should version the fold explicitly and make **the fold version part of the committed law.**

URLs: <https://spec.matrix.org/unstable/rooms/v12/> · MSC1442 (Erik Johnston) <https://github.com/matrix-org/matrix-spec-proposals/blob/old_master/proposals/1442-state-resolution.md> · explainer (Neil Alexander) <https://matrix.org/docs/older/stateres-v2/> · <https://matrix.org/blog/2025/08/project-hydra-improving-state-res/>

## 5.3 Keyhive (Ink & Switch) — "convergent capabilities"

**Status** [V]: <https://github.com/inkandswitch/keyhive> created 2024-08-13, **last commit 2026-07-09**, 232 stars, not archived. Public notebook <https://www.inkandswitch.com/keyhive/notebook/> has six chapters, **last dated 2025-03-13** — code live, public write-up quiet ~16 months. People: **Brooklyn Zelenka** (also UCAN's author), **John Mumm**, **Alex Good**.

Substance [S + V]:
- **"Convergent capabilities" (concap)** — positioned *between* object-capabilities and certificate-capabilities: "stateful delegation through CRDT state while remaining partition-tolerant." Group membership is **derived from delegation chains**; a delegation is revocable by the original delegator or any member with "manage" authority. Documents are identified by a public key and delegate control over themselves; documents can contain documents.
- Explicitly rejects the two incumbent answers Custos also rejects: routing auth through a cloud server, or **"storing access control policies on a blockchain."**
- Names the status quo it replaces: **"Swiss number" / "Rumpelstiltskin"** security — knowing the doc ID *is* the permission.
- **BeeKEM**, a concurrency-friendly continuous group key agreement, because TreeKEM "depends on a central server to create a total order of operations and pick winners among concurrent operations." Concurrent mutual revocation handled by **"conflict keys"**: "the remove paths are blanked after all other concurrent operations are merged."
- Claims **"every replica computes identical authorization decisions through causal ordering rather than total ordering."**
- Co-designed with Automerge's next sync protocol (**Beelay**); also used in Ink & Switch's **GAIOS** under the ARIA Safeguarded AI programme.

> **Supplies:** (a) the best-articulated *design vocabulary* for capability semantics that survive partition — **steal "convergent capability" as a term of art**; (b) a worked answer to the read-side problem Custos may not have scoped: authorisation predicates say who *may* read, but confidentiality needs concurrent group key agreement — if Custos ever governs *encrypted* domain data, BeeKEM/DCGKA is the shape of the answer; (c) a differentiation opportunity, since Keyhive's authority derivation is bespoke and protocol-embedded, whereas Custos declares law as a committed artifact.

Related: **DCGKA** (Decentralized Continuous Group Key Agreement, associated with Kleppmann) is the linear-cost design BeeKEM improves on. [S]

## 5.4 p2panda-auth — a published, reasoned convergent access-control CRDT with a *pluggable resolver*

Two engineering write-ups, both [V] read in full:

**(a) "Access Control in Decentralised Systems," 2025-07-28** — <https://p2panda.org/2025/07/28/access-control.html> — by **glyph & sam**, edited by **Dr. Erick Lavoie**.
- A distributed ACL with **cumulative access levels (Pull, Read, Write, Manage)** plus optional application-specific conditions. Notably **not** capability-based: managers hold the exclusive right to modify group state.
- Operations form a **causal DAG via `previous` and `dependencies` fields**, enabling concurrency detection without total order.
- **A pluggable `Resolver` trait** — conflict-resolution policy is a *parameter of the system*, with an initial **"strong removal"** implementation.
- On mutual manager removal, verbatim: **"all removals are applied to the group state but any other concurrent actions by those members are invalidated"**; "Removal or demotion of a manager causes any concurrent actions by that member to be invalidated," with transitive invalidation cascading through dependent operations.
- Nested groups (members may be groups). Cites as prior art: localfirst/auth, Keyhive, Meadowcap, Cryptree.

**(b) "Notes on building a convergent, offline-first Access Control CRDT," 2025-08-27** — <https://p2panda.org/2025/08/27/notes-convergent-access-control-crdt.html> — by **adz**. Enumerates the concurrent-revocation tie-break menu verbatim:
- **Seniority ranking:** "'older' members win over concurrent removals. Prone to Sibyl attack so further mitigation is required."
- **Remove both:** "Solution for byzantine scenarios where a group was 'infiltrated'."
- **Higher hash wins:** "Removal operation with 'higher hash' value wins deterministically."
- And the doctrine Custos should adopt: **"Which strategy to pick should be decided by a threat model and how well it can be communicated to the users."**

**Status** [V]: <https://github.com/p2panda/p2panda> pushed **2026-07-29** (today), **v0.7.0 released 2026-07-07**, 522 stars. Very much alive.

> **Supplies: the closest existing artifact to "domain law as a *parameter*."** The `Resolver` trait is the pattern Custos wants: **the fold is fixed; the tie-break policy is declared; different domains may declare different policies while every observer of a given domain still converges.** Plus an honest enumeration of the four possible answers to mutual revocation with each one's attack. And note precisely *how* "strong removal" works: it expands the **scope of effect** of a revocation to include *concurrent* events, not merely successors. That is the mechanism Custos will need, and it is exactly the non-monotone step.

## 5.5 The non-monotonicity problem, named: "duelling admins"

**Kleppmann's PaPoC 2025 keynote — "Byzantine Eventual Consistency and Local-First Access Control,"** opening keynote, 31 Mar 2025, Rotterdam. <https://martin.kleppmann.com/2025/03/31/papoc-keynote-byzantine.html> [S]. Framing: CRDTs delivered eventual consistency without a central server "but have ignored the question of which peers are allowed to update the state"; doing access control without reintroducing a server means the ACL is itself a CRDT that must resist malicious manipulation. Two ideas downstream papers cite it for:
1. **Seniority ranking** — execute the more senior admin's operations first. **Attack:** a revoked Admin A backdates a revocation against B to fake mutual concurrent revocation, thereby rolling back their own revocation by abusing seniority.
2. **Discursive / concurrent-scope revocation** — because Byzantine peers can backdate to exclude a revocation from an event's precursors, "authorized revoke events must affect the authorization of **concurrent** events as well as successors, in order to make backdating ineffective."

**GAP WORTH ACTING ON** [S]: his Cambridge student-projects page (<https://martin.kleppmann.com/student-projects.html>) states there is a **draft paper "available on request"** — i.e. the canonical local-first authorization paper may still be unpublished. If so, **there is no settled published standard here** and Custos has an opening. Worth an email.

**ERA: Epoch-Resolved Arbitration for Duelling Admins in Group Management CRDTs** — [V] arXiv:2601.22963, **Kegan Dougal** (single author; [R, unverified] Dougal is a Matrix/Element engineer), submitted 30 Jan 2026, revised 8 Apr 2026. Verbatim fragments: the problem is "two equally permissioned admins concurrently revoke each other's permissions"; ERA adds "optional 'epoch events'" arbitrating asynchronously in batches, introducing **"a bounded total order within epochs"**; and critically it asserts **"an external arbiter is required to order concurrent events"** to stop Byzantine exploitation. Framing of the underlying issue [S]: "revocation is problematic because it is a **non-monotonic** operation, meaning some previously authorised events may need to be rolled back due to concurrent revocations."

> **The hard warning.** Revocation and expiry are non-monotone, so CALM says no coordination-free implementation of *arbitrary* revocation semantics exists. The field's four responses:
> (i) **Make the effect monotone** — grow-only revocation set, tombstones, 2P-set, remove-wins.
> (ii) **Expand revocation's scope of effect to concurrent events** — Kleppmann's "discursive," p2panda's "strong removal." Monotone in the union of revocations, non-monotone in the *derived* authority set.
> (iii) **Deterministic arbitrary tie-break** (higher hash) — monotone and convergent but **semantically indefensible in a legal setting**, which matters more for Custos than for a chat app.
> (iv) **Admit a bounded arbiter** (ERA) — abandons coordination-freedom.
>
> **Custos must pick one explicitly and say so in the spec, because ERA's claim that "an external arbiter is required" is a direct, citable challenge to the no-global-consensus commitment.** The reading most consistent with Custos's philosophy is (ii), with (i) as the mechanism that makes (ii) implementable as a fold.

Also here:
- **"Decoupling Trust in Byzantine CRDTs: Fine-grained Post-Compromise Handling without Breaking Causality"** — [V] arXiv:2606.31759, **Amos Brocco**, v1 30 Jun 2026. Separates *identity-based* from *content-based* trust; uses "deterministic reconstruction" so replicas "preserve previously accepted updates while enabling selective inclusion or exclusion based on both the originating identity… and the semantics of individual updates." Its problem statement [S] is worth quoting to stakeholders: the causal partial order "doesn't determine which updates fall before or after the point a participant becomes untrusted — updates generated offline may surface later and interleave with newer ones, so **neither timestamps nor causal ordering alone let replicas reliably decide from which point revocation applies.**" **No mention of KERI [V]** — and this is exactly where KERI's pre-rotation and key event *receipts* give Custos an advantage this literature lacks. **Flag as a Custos contribution, not a gap.**
- **"A Composable CRDT Layer for Byzantine-Resilient Deterministic Reconstruction"** — arXiv:2606.18966 [S, unverified].
- **Policy-CRDT: … Remove-Wins Strategy for Convergent Access Control in Asynchronous Environments** — Dec 2025 preprint, <https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202512.1467> [S]. A 2P-Set-based join-semilattice of (added, revoked) monotonically growing policy-ID sets — response (i) in its simplest form. Low novelty; useful as the trivially-correct baseline to point at.
- **Rault, Ignat, Perrin, "Access Control based on CRDTs for Collaborative Distributed Applications,"** TrustCom 2023, 1369–1376, IEEE 10538952, open PDF <https://inria.hal.science/hal-04224855> [S]. Targets **multiple concurrent administrators** with "a specialized causal model that minimizes concurrency"; honest-but-curious rather than Byzantine; covers delegation, revocation and **compensating actions** after access-control conflicts. Follow-on: **Pierre-Antoine Rault's 2024 PhD, "Access control mechanisms for collaborative systems without central authority," Université de Lorraine** — likely the most complete single document on this subproblem.

## 5.6 CALM / Bloom^L / Hydro — the theory that says whether your fold is safe

**CALM theorem:** "A program has a consistent, coordination-free distributed implementation **iff** it is monotonic." Conjectured by **Hellerstein** (PODS 2010 keynote); proved for queries by **Ameloot, Neven & Van den Bussche** via relational transducer networks. Canonical accessible statement: **Hellerstein & Alvaro, "Keeping CALM: When Distributed Consistency is Easy," arXiv:1901.01930**, later CACM doi:10.1145/3369736 [S]. Crucially, **consistency there is defined as confluence** — "an operation is confluent if it produces the same set of outputs for any non-deterministic ordering and batching of a set of inputs." **That is Custos's replayability property, stated as a formal predicate on the fold.**

**The refinement that matters more than the base theorem** [S]: *"Weaker Forms of Monotonicity for Declarative Networking: A More Fine-Grained Answer to the CALM-Conjecture"* (ACM TODS 40(4), doi:10.1145/2809784; earlier arXiv:1202.0242) shows that when nodes can check whether a ground atom is in their local **"scope,"** seemingly non-monotone reasoning *can* still be done coordination-free. **This is the most directly load-bearing result found for Custos's revocation problem** — the formal handle on "my judgment is correct with respect to *my* evidence base." Pair with the KIT "knowledge and belief" framing. Also: *"A Generalized CALM Theorem for Non-Deterministic Computation in Asynchronous Distributed Systems,"* Information Systems 2026, doi S0306437926000050; *"A Preliminary Model of Coordination-free Consistency,"* arXiv:2504.01141.

**Bloom^L / lattices:** **Conway, Marczak, Alvaro, Hellerstein, Maier, "Logic and Lattices for Distributed Programming," SoCC '12**, doi:10.1145/2391229.2391230, PDF <https://dsf.berkeley.edu/papers/UCB-lattice-tr.pdf> [S]. Generalises Bloom to lattices, extends CALM analysis to whole programs containing arbitrary lattices, and its compiler can **automatically verify** the case studies are eventually consistent. Ancestors: **Dedalus** ("Datalog in time and space," *Datalog Reloaded*, 2011); **"Consistency Analysis in Bloom: a CALM and Collected Approach," CIDR 2011**, which introduced **"points of order"** — the code locations where coordination must be injected; **BOOM Analytics, EuroSys 2010**. [R] Bud (the Ruby Bloom runtime) is long dormant — verify before citing as tooling.

**Hydro** (the live successor): <https://hydro.run> · <https://github.com/hydro-project/hydro> — [V] pushed **2026-07-29**, 1271 stars, alpha releases 2026-07-14. Led by Hellerstein; [S] several Hydro graduates joined AWS in 2025 to continue development in production. Directly relevant: **Power, Koutris & Hellerstein on "free termination"** (Berkeley TR **EECS-2025-7**) — when may a node *unilaterally decide it is done* without coordination — which "develops a semiautomata-based model bridging the relational transducer model of CALM to algebraic models like CRDTs." Also **"Wrapping Rings in Lattices: An Algebraic Symbiosis of Incremental View Maintenance and Eventual Consistency," PaPoC 2024**.

> **Supplies:** (1) a named property — **confluence** — to claim, and a **decision procedure** to claim it with, rather than asserting replayability; (2) **"free termination"** is the missing piece of "a stranger recomputes the same judgment" — the stranger needs to know *when it may stop waiting for more evidence*, and this is where the answer lives; (3) the blunt verdict that **your revocation and expiry predicates are non-monotone, so by CALM there is no coordination-free implementation of them in full generality.** Custos must either weaken the semantics (scope-relative monotonicity), make the effect monotone (tombstones/2P-set), or admit coordination. **Saying nothing is not an option.**

## 5.7 Causal stability / pure op-based CRDTs — the "sealing" primitive

**Baquero, Almeida & Shoker**, "Making operation-based CRDTs operation-based," **DAIS 2014**; and **"Pure Operation-Based Replicated Data Types," arXiv:1710.04469** (2017) [S]. State splits into a **PO-Log** plus a causality-stripped sequential component, with **causal stability** supplied by a **tagged causal stable broadcast (TCSB)** middleware. Definition: an operation is causally stable at a replica once **there can be no further operations concurrent to it** — every future operation that replica sees will causally follow it. Once stable, causal metadata is stripped. The canonical worked example is Custos's problem: **"in a remove-wins set, a remove wins over a concurrent add of the same element and must be kept in the PO-Log for some time in case a concurrent add shows up — but once the remove becomes causally stable, it can be discarded."**

Caveat [S]: causal stability is "tricky to ensure in a collaborative app where replicas come and go" — the stability frontier can **regress** under dynamic membership, so the safe cut is not itself monotone in an open-membership system. Survey: **"Approaches to Conflict-free Replicated Data Types," ACM Computing Surveys**, doi:10.1145/3695249, which formalises a `stabilize` function. Follow-ons: Bauwens & Gonzalez Boix, **"Nested Pure Operation-Based CRDTs," ECOOP 2023**, LIPIcs 263, 2:1–2:26.

> **Supplies: the rigorous version of "sealing."** The exact condition under which a Custos judgment becomes **final** rather than merely current — and therefore when the fold's output may be published as settled, when history may be compacted, and when a non-monotone rule (remove-wins revocation) becomes safe to apply irrevocably. It also warns that in an open domain with joining/leaving participants **you cannot compute stability without a membership bound** — itself a governance rule, i.e. a chicken-and-egg dependency Custos should design explicitly. **KERI's declared witness/quorum sets give Custos a membership bound the CRDT literature has to invent — another genuine advantage.**

## 5.8 Biscuit — Datalog authorization predicates in an offline-verifiable, attenuable token

**What/who** [S]: created by **Geoffroy Couprie** (Clever Cloud; also author of `nom`, `sozu`). Merges JWT-style public-key signatures with **Macaroons-style offline attenuation**, but replaces opaque byte-array caveats with **a real Datalog policy language**. Stated rationale: Macaroon caveats are "essentially a byte array requiring your own encoding/testing system," whereas Datalog "can also represent more complex rules such as role-based access control, delegation, and hierarchies." Policies may be **carried by the token or supplied at verification**; Protobuf-encoded; signature check + deserialize + policy evaluation typically **under 1 ms**. Attenuation: a holder appends a block of additional checks; blocks cannot be removed without invalidating the signature; attenuation uses ephemeral keypairs destroyed immediately after use; only the authority block uses the root key, which is never carried in the token. No authorization server is queried.

**Status** [V]: now an **Eclipse Foundation project** — <https://github.com/eclipse-biscuit/biscuit>, 1138 stars, **last push 2025-10-21**, **latest release v3.3 on 2024-12-17**. Spec: <https://doc.biscuitsec.org/reference/specifications.html>. Production users [S]: Clever Cloud (Pulsar namespaces), Space And Time, nixbuild.net. **Mature and real but slowing** — 19 months since a release.

> **Supplies: the best existing answer to "how do you serialise a machine-evaluable predicate as a signed, content-addressed, verifiable artifact."** Specifically (a) the **monotone attenuation discipline** — appended blocks may only *narrow*, never widen, a structural monotonicity guarantee at the artifact level that Custos's law-amendment events could adopt directly; (b) a **compact binary encoding of Datalog** with a versioned spec and multi-language implementations, so Custos need not invent a predicate wire format; (c) the split between *token-carried* and *verifier-supplied* policy, which maps onto Custos's split between committed domain law and observer-local evidence.
>
> **Does not supply:** Biscuit's Datalog is evaluated against facts supplied at one instant by one verifier. **No partial order, no growing evidence base, no revocation beyond expiry** (out of scope by design). Right *predicate language* prior art; wrong *distributed evaluation* prior art.
>
> **[R] — HIGH-VALUE ITEM TO VERIFY:** my recollection is that Biscuit's Datalog is deliberately restricted (**no negation**), which if still true would make it **monotone by construction** — a very attractive property for Custos. **Check this against the current spec; it may be the single most useful concrete detail in this report.**

## 5.9 UCAN 1.0 — the explicitly "local-first auth" certificate-chain system

**Status** [V, from <https://github.com/ucan-wg/spec/blob/main/README.md>]: **v1.0.0, described as a stable specification.** Repo created 2021-11-25, **last push 2026-07-08**, 285 stars, **no GitHub releases cut**. Split into sub-specs `ucan-wg/delegation`, `ucan-wg/invocation`, `ucan-wg/revocation` (**revocation repo last push 2025-07-14, 3 stars — a telling signal**). Lineage: Brooklyn Zelenka / Fission → UCAN WG. Site <https://ucan.xyz>.

Verbatim/near-verbatim [V]:
- Self-description: "trustless, secure, **local-first**, user-originated, distributed authorization," achieving public verifiability with **late-bound certificate chains**, principals as DIDs, usable "offline, online, fully P2P, federated, or with central servers."
- Lineage: "Since offline operation and self-verifiability are requirements, UCAN adopts an approach closely related to **SPKI**, following the **'capabilities as certificates'** model with extensions for revocation and stateful capabilities."
- Revocation: **"The ability to revoke some or all downstream UCANs exists as a last resort."** Irreversible: "When a validator learns of a revocation by UCAN CID, that UCAN and all of its derivatives in the cache MUST be marked invalid." Aside from revocation, "capability validation is **idempotent** — marking a CID… as valid acts as memoization."
- Monotone authority merge: **"Capability authorities can be combined in any order, with the result always being at least as broad as each."** (A join-semilattice on authority.)
- Attenuation: each direct delegation "MUST either directly restate or attenuate (diminish) its capabilities," with the single exception of **rights amplification**, where a delegation may be proven by multiple witnesses of different types.
- Expiry: `exp` is REQUIRED (may be null); "Following the principle of least authority, it is RECOMMENDED to give a timestamp expiry."

> **Supplies:** (a) the cleanest published statement that **revocation is architecturally an admission of failure** in a self-certifying system — "last resort," with expiry preferred. Custos should decide whether it agrees. (b) **Irreversible + transitive-to-derivatives revocation with memoized validation** is directly implementable and *is* monotone (the known-revoked set only grows), at the cost that judgment is **evidence-base-relative rather than absolute** — an observer who has not seen the revocation computes a different answer. That is Custos's honesty problem in miniature, and **UCAN's answer is "accept it."** (c) The explicit monotone join on authority is a lattice you can reuse.
>
> **Caution:** the WG warns that older write-ups (JWT-framed material, `ts-ucan`, AWAKE) describe an obsolete design; v1 dropped JWT framing for late-bound certificate chains. **Do not cite pre-2023 UCAN material.**

## 5.10 Catala — prioritised default logic as the computational shape of statute

**Who/what:** **Denis Merigoux, Nicolas Chataing, Jonathan Protzenko** (Inria / Microsoft Research). **"Catala: A Programming Language for the Law," ICFP/PACMPL 2021**, doi:10.1145/3473582, arXiv:2103.03198.

**Computational model — the reason it matters** [S]: Catala's semantics is **prioritised default logic**. Defaults have the form `a : b⃗ᵢ / c` — if `a` holds and the `b⃗ᵢ` are consistent with known facts, then `c`; `a` is the precondition, the `b⃗ᵢ` are **exceptions blocking the default**. **Prioritisation adds a strict partial order over defaults** to resolve simultaneously-admissible conflicts. Adopted on **Sarah Lawsky's** argument that this flavour of default logic best expresses law. Catala implements a restricted form: "each definition is its own world equipped with a static pre-order." Claimed to be **the only programming language embedding default logic as a first-class feature.** Fully declarative and out-of-order (the compiler computes the dependency order). And: **"since the law has no general recursion, no fixed points are needed."**

**Status** [V]: <https://github.com/CatalaLang/catala> — **2351 stars**, **last push 2026-07-29** (today), **release 1.2.1 on 2026-07-06**, nightly builds daily. Institutional trajectory [S]: developed at Inria since 2019 under **Programme Apollo**; **CNAF (French family-benefits agency) adopted Catala as its reference technology for social benefit computation** after a 2024–25 PoC, and in **June 2026 CNAF and Inria signed a partnership**; a 2026 ASP experiment covers CAP agricultural aid; **CATALA won the first Collège des sociétés savantes / CNRS / France Universités interdisciplinarity prize in 2026.** Related: **AVoCat** (automatic verification of Catala programs); **CUTECat: Concolic Execution for Computational Law** (arXiv:2410.18212), which handles default logic and generated hundreds of thousands of tests against French housing benefits and **US IRC §132**.

> **Supplies: the answer to "what should a Custos predicate *look like*."** A flat conjunction of guards is the wrong shape for law; law is **defaults + exceptions + a partial order of priority among them.** Three specific gifts:
> 1. **A partial order over rules is already the native structure of legal reasoning.** Custos has a partial order over *events*; Catala has a partial order over *norms*. **Composing the two — priority-ordered defaults evaluated against a causally-ordered evidence base — is, as far as either researcher could find, unoccupied territory, and is arguably Custos's core novel contribution.**
> 2. **"The law has no general recursion, so no fixed points are needed"** is your licence to make the fold **total and terminating by construction** — necessary for "recomputes byte for byte" to be safe against *adversarial* law. (Note this independently confirms Ergo's restriction in §2.3.)
> 3. **Catala's `exception`/`definition` structure is monotone-friendly**: adding a higher-priority exception does not require retracting facts, it re-derives. That may be the clean way to express Custos revocation **without a non-monotone retraction step** — a specific, testable design hypothesis.
>
> **Limitation:** Catala has **no distribution model whatsoever** — single evaluator, complete inputs, no partial order over evidence, no notion of an observer. It is a compiler, not a protocol.

## 5.11 LegalRuleML + defeasible logic for law (Governatori et al.)

**LegalRuleML Core Specification v1.0 — a full OASIS Standard, approved 30 August 2021.** Editors: **Monica Palmirani, Guido Governatori, Tara Athan, Harold Boley, Adrian Paschke, Adam Wyner**. XML Schema + Relax NG, based on Consumer RuleML 1.02. Four Statements of Use (Livio Robaldo, Swansea, CSIRO Data61, CIRSFID-AlmaAI). <https://www.oasis-open.org/standard/legalruleml-core-specification-version-1-0-oasis-standard/> [S]

Mechanisms to mine [S]:
- **Defeasibility via an explicit superiority relation**, expressed with the **`<Overrides>` element** defining superiority between statements.
- **Rule strength as a first-class, time-varying parameter**: status (*strict / defeasible / defeater*), validity (**repealed / annulled / suspended**), and jurisdiction — all of which "can vary over time."
- **Deliberately logic-agnostic**: independent of any specific legal ontology or logic framework, with an **IRI-based mechanism to point at an external framework** — semantics are *pluggable*.
- Deontic operators plus **penalty and reparation** concepts.

References: Athan, Boley, Governatori, Palmirani, Paschke, Wyner, **"OASIS LegalRuleML," ICAIL 2013**, 3–12 (<http://www.governatori.net/papers/2013/icail2013legalruleml.pdf>); **"LegalRuleML: Design Principles and Foundations," Reasoning Web 2015**, LNCS 9203, 151–188; **"Enabling Reasoning with LegalRuleML," arXiv:1711.06128**; **"Bridging between LegalRuleML and TPTP for Automated Normative Reasoning," arXiv:2209.05090**.

**Tooling — mostly legacy** [S]: **SPINdle** (Java defeasible-logic reasoner, >1M rules, modal defeasible logics, XML theories; contact Dr Ho-Pun Lam; CSIRO Data61 page c.2017 + SourceForge) shows **multiple indicators of dormancy — do not assume it runs**. **DR-Prolog / DR-Device** (Antoniou et al., IEEE TKDE 19(2):233–245, 2007). SPINdle and Delores are the only bottom-up/forward-chaining DL implementations and are **propositional only**. **Regorous** — Governatori's business-process compliance checker over **defeasible deontic logic** (FCL → PCL). Best current survey: **"Three Decades of Formal Methods in Business Process Compliance," arXiv:2410.10906**.

> **Supplies:** (a) **an existing OASIS-standard vocabulary for exactly the metadata Custos's governance events must carry** — which rule overrides which, whether a rule is strict/defeasible/defeater, and whether it is **repealed vs annulled vs suspended**, and from when. **Do not invent this taxonomy.** The *annulled* (never valid, retroactive) vs *repealed* (valid until now, prospective) distinction is precisely the retroactivity semantics a fold must get right, and it is already standardised. (b) The **pluggable-logic-via-IRI** pattern is the right precedent for letting a domain declare *which* evaluation semantics its law uses while the log format stays fixed. (c) A warning: defeasible logic's reasoning assumes a complete, static theory and a single reasoner. **None of this tooling has any concept of incomplete or growing evidence** — which is why "defeasible reasoning under incomplete evidence" is aspirational in legal informatics rather than delivered.

## 5.14 Two cross-cutting findings from §5 that need action

**(a) The "state = fold over an append-only log" framing may be patent-encumbered.** [S, **and explicitly unverified against USPTO — check before relying on this**] No source uses "the law is a fold," but the framing is textbook event sourcing *and* appears in **US Patent 11,216,444 ("Scalable event sourcing datastore")**, which states that current state "is computed by folding over the event log." A live application, **US App. 19/561,229** ("Append-only governance audit ledger"), reportedly claims logging the decision input, **the policy resolution path (policies consulted, rules that fired, precedence order applied)**, lineage state at decision time, outcome, and timestamp/logical counter, arguing that distributed event-sourcing logs "provide replication and ordering but generally lack tamper-evident commitments." **If these hold up, Custos's novelty must be located elsewhere than the fold** — plausibly in (i) observer-local judgment correctness, (ii) bitemporal/retroactivity semantics (old facts under new law), (iii) tamper-evidence of the **law artifact** rather than of decisions, and (iv) the KERI key-state substrate.

Recent agentic-governance preprints already occupy the naive framing [S]: **OpenKedge** (arXiv:2604.08601) defines `S_n = f(E_≤n)` with determinism/auditability/replayability; **Faramesh** (arXiv:2601.17744) distinguishes **replay** (pure, analytical) from **re-execution** (irreversible side effects) — a distinction Custos should adopt verbatim; **"The Log is the Agent"** (arXiv:2605.21997) states the determinism contract crisply: replay is sound only if behaviour bodies are deterministic functions of their inputs — **no wall-clock, no randomness, no fresh UUIDs, no I/O, no mutable globals.** That last list belongs in the Custos conformance section as-is.

**(b) The known failure mode of "law as fold" is versioned rules.** [S] If the fold's projection depends on values that change over time (the classic: computing tax against a rate that later changes), naive replay is wrong. **Custos must decide explicitly whether the fold evaluates (i) under the law as it stood at each event's commit point, or (ii) under current law across all history** — and this is where LegalRuleML's *repealed / annulled / suspended* distinction earns its keep.

# 6. What NOT to borrow, and why

Assumptions that ship silently with these designs and would break a no-global-consensus system. Organised by where the assumption comes from, because that is how you will catch it in review.

## 6.1 From the Ricardian/legal-document tradition

1. **"One document that is simultaneously the prose and the machine input."** This is the folklore reading of the Ricardian contract, and **its inventor recommends against it**: prose and code are "so far apart, semantically, that treating them as one document remains an open research project" (§1.5a). Borrow the *mutual hash reference* instead. The failure mode of the blended artifact is that neither audience can be served without parsing the other's syntax — and it is the reason Lexon and OpenLaw both stalled.
2. **Amendment as replacement.** The Ricardian contract has no versioning: "any change to the contract creates a new hash, and that new hash is not the one which the users have or value" (§1.3c). That is correct for a per-instrument contract and **fatal for domain law**, because a norm applies to classes of parties over time. Custos's whole reason to exist is that the `r`-field model cannot amend without mass re-issuance. Do not inherit the immutability-by-hash-of-the-whole; inherit hash-of-the-version plus an ordered amendment sequence.
3. **"Machine-readable" meaning "parseable."** ACDC says the rules section is "human and machine readable"; the `l` field is a bare prose string (§1.7). If Custos uses the same words for a genuinely different property, reviewers will conflate them and the standard's central claim will be read as already satisfied. **Use a different word — evaluable, decidable, computable — and define it.**
4. **Assent as the binding mechanism.** ACDC rules bind because the disclosee accepted them in an exchange ceremony. **Domain law binds parties who never assented and were not present.** Every mechanism inherited from the contract layer must be re-justified on a non-assent basis, or the standard will be arguing for jurisdiction using the vocabulary of contract.
5. **Single-issuer authorship.** A Ricardian contract is signed by *the issuer*. Domain law is authored by a body under a threshold rule, amendable by a different threshold rule, and its authorship rule is itself part of the law. Nothing in the Ricardian lineage models this.

## 6.2 From the legal-DSL tradition (Ergo, Catala, Stipula, Symboleo, LegalRuleML)

6. **A single evaluator with complete inputs.** This is the *universal* unstated premise of the entire legal-informatics literature. Catala, Ergo, Stipula, Symboleo, LegalRuleML, SPINdle, DR-Prolog, Blawx — every one assumes the reasoner has the whole theory and all the facts. **There is no observer, no partial evidence, no "I have not seen that event yet."** Importing their evaluation model without adding an evidence-base parameter silently converts "the law says X" into "the law says X *and I know everything*," which is the exact claim Custos cannot make.
7. **Non-monotone retraction as a primitive.** Defeasible logic's superiority relation and Catala's exceptions are fine (they re-derive rather than retract). But LegalRuleML's *repealed/annulled/suspended* and any rule with a genuine retraction step are non-monotone, and by CALM (§5.6) have no coordination-free implementation in general. Borrow the **taxonomy**; do not borrow the assumption that applying it is free.
8. **"Compile to a general-purpose language."** Accord Project's Ergo lost to TypeScript for good reasons (§2.4) — none of which apply to Custos, because **Accord Project never needed byte-identical third-party replay.** A TypeScript logic layer is not a specification; two conformant implementations can differ. Expect this pressure and have the answer ready.
9. **Immutability of the model as the integrity story.** Concerto/Ergo treat the type model as fixed at template-authoring time. Custos's schemas evolve under governance, and "which schema was current" is itself a governed fact.

## 6.3 From the smart-contract / DAO tradition

10. **"There is a current state."** The deepest one; everything else follows. Replace with "there is a *causally-closed prefix named by a digest*," and **make it a type-level property the evaluator enforces** rather than a convention (§4.5).
11. **A shared clock** — `block.timestamp`, `block.number`, slot numbers, ERC-6372 units, `after(n)`/`older(n)`. Replace with log position for procedural delays, witnessed/anchored time where wall-clock is unavoidable (with Ceramic's secret-anchor mitigation built in), and Marlowe-style mandatory default continuations so the fold is total at every waiting point.
12. **Transferable, fungible voting weight.** Token voting is a balance invariant, therefore provably not enforceable without consensus (§4.1). Replace with roster/membership weight fixed by a prior event digest. **There is no clever workaround; do not look for one.**
13. **Time-keyed snapshots.** Replace with digest-keyed snapshots — a strict improvement, not a workaround.
14. **Revert / rollback / `require` atomicity.** Append-only means never un-happen. The fold must be total, invalid → no-op + fault record, **with a distinct third outcome for insufficient evidence** (Holochain's `UnresolvedDependencies`).
15. **Gas as the termination bound.** No meter, no payer, no consensus to price it. Termination must be static and derivable from the program text (§4.9).
16. **Reentrancy guards and checks-effects-interactions.** A category error in a pure fold; importing the ceremony misleads readers about the execution model.
17. **Proxy/`delegatecall` upgrade machinery.** Unnecessary — but **do not skip the question it hides.** Specify retroactive vs prospective amendment (recommendation: law-in-force-at-event-position), and handle the recursive case the way Tezos does.
18. **First-come-first-served mechanics.** Order-sensitive. Single-writer or monotone-plus-tie-break — **and tell users the second option is a policy change, not a refactor.**
19. **Cross-domain atomicity.** Stingray names multi-party operations as the boundary; Canton buys atomicity with exactly the per-domain total order you lack. Offer eventual coordination and say so plainly.
20. **Budget invariants with multiple spenders.** Forbidden by Theorem 3.1. Partition into single-writer shares or use bounded counters.
21. **Destructive deletion.** Attributed negation plus expiry (AT Protocol's `neg`/`exp`), never a destructive delete; **retraction must not imply the inverse assertion.**
22. **"Code is law" absolutism.** The DAO forked; Parity did not and 513,774 ETH are still gone; the L2s institutionalised pre-authorised emergency councils. Put the emergency power *in the law* — named holders, bounded scope, mandatory delay, transparency, periodic re-election — and lean on the structural fact that an append-only log separates the power to change future rules from the power to rewrite history.
23. **Prescriptive, feature-rich governance frameworks.** Aragon retired Voice, Govern and Court for being "too prescriptive and complex for broad adoption," and its Association dissolved while aragonOS v1 still quietly runs Lido and Curve. **Ship a small language with strong analysis obligations — Miniscript's scope, not Solidity's — and design the standard to outlive whoever publishes it.**

## 6.4 From the local-first / CRDT tradition

24. **Arbitrary deterministic tie-breaks.** "Higher hash wins" is monotone, convergent, and **semantically indefensible in a legal setting.** It is fine for a chat app's power levels; it is not fine for a rule that decides who was in breach. If Custos uses a hash tie-break anywhere, that must be a *declared policy* of the domain with a stated justification, not a protocol default hidden in the merge function (§5.4, §5.5).
25. **Causal stability as a free primitive.** Stability lets you finalise and compact — but the stability frontier **regresses under dynamic membership** (§5.7). Do not build finality on stability without first fixing a membership bound; happily, KERI's witness/quorum declarations give you one.
26. **Seniority ranking for concurrent revocation.** Kleppmann's own analysis of it is an attack: a revoked admin backdates a revocation to fake mutual concurrency and thereby rolls back their own revocation (§5.5). Do not adopt seniority without the anti-backdating countermeasure.
27. **"Every replica computes identical authorization decisions."** Keyhive claims this; it is true only *given the same evidence*. Custos must not reproduce the claim without the qualifier, because the qualifier is the whole subject. **The KIT formulation — correct "to the best of knowledge and belief locally available" — is the honest version, and it is peer-reviewed** (§5.1).
28. **Informal specification with a reference implementation as the spec.** The 2026 Verus paper's opening observation is that **Matrix and Keyhive both do this** (§5.1). It is the norm in this space. It is also Custos's clearest differentiation opportunity, and abandoning it would forfeit the standard's main advantage.
29. **Blinded / partially-disclosed law.** ACDC's `u` nonce makes clause-level blinding available (§1.7). Two verifiers holding different subsets of the rule tree both verify — and both compute different judgments. **That is a split-view surface at clause granularity. It is not addressed anywhere in the literature I found. Decide explicitly whether domain-law predicates may be blinded, and if so, what a judgment over a partially-disclosed rule tree means.**

## 6.5 The one meta-warning

**Do not borrow the *silence*.** Every tradition surveyed here is silent about the thing the other tradition treats as central. The legal DSLs say nothing about observers; the CRDT work says nothing about what a rule *means*; the smart-contract work says nothing about either because consensus hid both questions. A Custos reviewer fluent in one tradition will read the standard's silence on that tradition's central question as an oversight. **State both explicitly: what a predicate means (Catala/LegalRuleML vocabulary) and what correctness means when evidence differs (KIT vocabulary).**

# 7. Synthesis: the gap, and what to steal

## 7.1 The gap, stated for the spec's related-work section

> **The distributed-systems literature has rigorous partial-order semantics with trivial rule languages. The legal-informatics literature has law-shaped rule languages with no distribution model at all. Nobody has joined them.**

Concretely: the strongest partial-order-native governance systems in existence — Matrix state resolution, Keyhive, p2panda-auth, the KIT EDP programme, Holochain validation callbacks, AT Protocol labels — have rule languages consisting of *membership plus three or four access levels*. The strongest legal rule languages — Catala's prioritised defaults, LegalRuleML's superiority relation and rule-status taxonomy, Stipula's deontic state machines, Symboleo's obligations-and-powers with independent lifecycles — assume a single evaluator with complete inputs and have **no** notion of an observer, a partial order, or growing evidence.

**Custos's defensible contribution is the join: priority-ordered defeasible norms evaluated as a confluent fold over a partially-ordered, self-certifying evidence base.** Two independent researchers, working different literatures, each concluded the intersection is unoccupied.

There is also a **positive asymmetry worth claiming**: two recent papers solving adjacent problems — Brocco's Byzantine-CRDT trust decoupling (arXiv:2606.31759) and did:crdt (arXiv:2606.16223) — **do not mention KERI at all**, and Brocco's stated open problem is that "neither timestamps nor causal ordering alone let replicas reliably decide from which point revocation applies." **KERI's pre-rotation, key event receipts and declared witness sets give Custos a partial answer that this literature is missing.** That is a contribution, not a gap — but it needs to be stated to be recognised.

## 7.2 Ten things to steal, in priority order

1. **Grigg's mutual-reference architecture and prose-leads precedence rule** (§1.5a–b). Two SAID-addressed objects with reciprocal digests; prose is the default authority and may elect a named predicate as dispositive within a named domain; a predicate can never elect anything. Cite Grigg for both.
2. **The digest-named causally-closed prefix as a type-level property** (§4.5). "The roster as of event D," not "at time T." Enforced by the evaluator, not by convention — Holochain's integrity-zome model is how (§4.3).
3. **Extend-Only Directed Posets as the formal type of the log** (arXiv:2304.04318, §5.1), so confluence of the fold becomes a theorem rather than an assertion — and ℐ-confluence (Kleppmann & Howard Thm 3.1, §4.1) as the **documented design gate** every admitted predicate must pass.
4. **Catala's prioritised defaults with exceptions, over a partial order of norms** (§5.10) as the shape of a Custos predicate — plus its licence to make the fold total (no general recursion, no fixed points), which independently agrees with Ergo, Pact, Marlowe, Simplicity, Miniscript and Nomos (§4.9).
5. **BIP 379's three-layer architecture and analysis obligations** (§4.2): human policy / typed analysable IR / canonical serialised form; and satisfiability, witness construction, cost prediction and resource-limit checking as *language requirements*, not tooling extras.
6. **Holochain's three-valued validation result** — `Valid` / `Invalid` / `UnresolvedDependencies` (§4.3, §4.4 item 7). The last value is what makes a fold over partial evidence total without lying. Plus **warrants** as monotone, gossipable, self-certifying proof of rule violation.
7. **LegalRuleML's `<Overrides>` and its repealed/annulled/suspended taxonomy** (§5.11), because the annulled-vs-repealed distinction *is* the retroactivity semantics, and it is already an OASIS standard. Paired with the explicit decision that the fold applies **law-in-force-at-event-position** (§4.4 item 10).
8. **Maker's spell shape** (§4.2): one signed atomic bundle of parameter deltas with declared delay, declared expiry, a cancellation window, and a pre-deployed emergency halt. None of the four brakes needs consensus. Plus **Polkadot's tracks** — different amendment classes get different thresholds and delays, declared as data.
9. **Interactive bisection as a judgment dispute protocol** (§4.8), and **proof-carrying validation as an optional profile** (§5.1). Together they answer "verify without replaying" from both ends: O(log n) interactive adjudication of disagreement, and O(1) succinct verification of agreement. Both make evidence availability a *soundness requirement*, not an operational nicety.
10. **AT Protocol's `neg` + `exp`, plus attribution by `src`** (§4.3): retract without asserting the inverse, expire without deleting, and make every judgment carry its authority so disagreement is legible rather than a fault. Plus **the deliberate flatness of `val`** as a lesson about predicate identifiers.

Honourable mentions: **CommonAccord's prose-object composition** as the answer to Grigg's unsolved "layering" problem (§3.2); **p2panda's pluggable `Resolver`** as the pattern for making tie-break policy part of the declared law (§5.4); **Moloch's ragequit** as exit-instead-of-agreement, the most philosophically aligned DAO primitive in existence (§4.2); **Symboleo's independent obligation and power lifecycles** as the right decomposition for concurrent norms (§3.3); **Canton's semantics-blind sequencer** as validation that an ordering authority can have zero semantic authority (§4.2); **Move's CI-gated minutes-not-hours verification** as the practice that actually produces verified code (§4.6).

## 7.3 Five open questions the literature does not answer for you

1. **When may an observer stop waiting for evidence and declare judgment final?** → the answers live in "free termination" (Power, Koutris, Hellerstein, UCB/EECS-2025-7), causal stability (Baquero/Almeida/Shoker), and threshold reads (Kuper) — §5.6, §5.7, §5.13. Nobody has assembled them for a governance setting.
2. **What does "correct" mean when observers hold different evidence?** → KIT's "to the best of knowledge and belief" (§5.1) and scope-based weakened monotonicity (ACM TODS 40(4), §5.6). The vocabulary exists; the application to law does not.
3. **Concurrent revocation of authority.** Open research per Kleppmann's PaPoC 2025 keynote; four competing answers, one 2026 paper (ERA) claiming an external arbiter is *necessary* (§5.5). **Ship a named limitation and a conservative default; do not ship an under-specified answer.**
4. **What does a judgment over a partially-disclosed rule tree mean?** ACDC's `u`-blinded clauses make this constructible today and nothing addresses it (§1.7, §6.4 item 29).
5. **Is the "law as a fold" framing patent-encumbered?** Two US patent references surfaced and **were not verified against USPTO** (§5.14a). Check before the framing becomes load-bearing in publicity; and note that even if it is, Custos's novelty plausibly sits elsewhere — observer-local correctness, retroactivity semantics, tamper-evidence of the law artifact rather than of decisions, and the KERI substrate.
